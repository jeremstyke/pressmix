Raw eggs in their shell usually stay good for **3 to 5 weeks in the fridge**, and hard-boiled eggs for **about one week**. How long they last depends on whether they were washed, how you store them and whether they are cooked. 

## Raw eggs in the shell

The shell has a natural protective layer called the **cuticle** (or bloom) that keeps bacteria out. What happens next depends on where you live:

- **United States, Canada, Japan, Australia**: eggs are washed before sale, which removes the cuticle. They **must be refrigerated** and keep for about 3 to 5 weeks.
- **United Kingdom and most of Europe**: eggs are not washed, so they are often sold unrefrigerated. Even so, keeping them in the fridge at home helps them stay fresh longer.

Wherever you live, **once an egg has been refrigerated, keep it cold**: condensation on the shell helps bacteria get through.

### Where to put eggs in the fridge

Store them **in their original carton on a middle shelf**, not in the door, which is the warmest part of the fridge. The carton also stops eggs from absorbing smells.

## Cooked eggs and egg dishes

Cooked eggs spoil faster than raw ones because cooking removes their protective layer.

- **Hard-boiled, in the shell**: about 1 week.
- **Hard-boiled, peeled**: best eaten within a few days.
- **Scrambled eggs, omelet**: 3 to 4 days.
- **Quiche and other egg-based dishes**: 3 to 4 days.
- **Raw yolks or whites, covered**: 2 to 4 days.

Never leave cooked eggs **at room temperature for more than 2 hours** (1 hour on a very hot day).

## How to read the date on the carton

Labels vary by country:

- **"Best before"**: the eggs are at their best quality until this date. They are often still safe for a while after, if they have been refrigerated.
- **"Sell by"**: a date for the store, not for you. Eggs normally keep for a few more weeks at home.
- **Pack date**: in the US, a three-digit number (from 001 to 365) shows the day of the year the eggs were packed.

When in doubt, follow the guidance of your local food safety agency.

## The float test: is my egg still good?

This simple test shows how old an egg is:

1. Fill a bowl with cold water.
2. Gently place the egg in it.
3. Watch what happens.

- **It sinks and lies flat**: very fresh.
- **It sinks but stands upright**: older, still fine to cook well.
- **It floats**: very old, better to throw it away.

Why it works: as an egg ages, moisture escapes through the shell and the **air pocket inside grows**, making it lighter. Keep in mind the float test measures age, **not bacteria**. Always crack the egg into a separate bowl: a bad smell or unusual color means it goes straight in the bin.

## Can you freeze eggs?

Yes, but **never in their shell**, as they can crack. Beat whole eggs lightly, or separate whites and yolks, then freeze them in an airtight container or an ice cube tray. Thaw them in the fridge and use them in cooked dishes.

## Tips to keep eggs fresh longer

- Do not wash eggs before storing them, unless local guidance says otherwise.
- Keep them pointed end down to keep the yolk centered.
- Throw away cracked or dirty eggs.
- Plan your meals so you use the oldest eggs first. A weekly plan is part of a [simple monthly budget](/en/how-to-make-a-monthly-budget/) and cuts food waste.

## Summary

Raw eggs keep for about 3 to 5 weeks in the fridge, hard-boiled eggs for about a week and egg dishes for 3 to 4 days. Store eggs in their carton on a middle shelf, use the float test to check their age and always smell them after cracking. When an egg looks or smells wrong, do not take the risk.
