To chase an unpaid invoice, **check that the invoice is correct and was received**, then send a **friendly reminder** a few days after the due date, a **firmer follow-up** after one to two weeks, **call** the client, and finally send a **formal letter before action** stating a deadline and your legal options (late payment interest, small claims court). Keep every exchange in writing and stay professional: most late payments are resolved by the second or third reminder.

## Before chasing: check your own invoice

Many late payments come from a problem on the supplier's side. Before sending any reminder, check:

- the invoice was sent to the **right person** (often accounts payable, not your contact);
- it includes everything the client needs to process it: **invoice number, date, purchase order (PO) number if required, your bank details, the due date**;
- the **amount** matches the quote or contract;
- the **payment terms** are clearly stated (for example "payment due within 30 days of the invoice date").

If you are not sure your invoices are complete, see our guide on [how to write an invoice](/en/how-to-write-an-invoice/).

## The step-by-step follow-up process

Here is a timeline that works for most freelancers and small businesses. Adjust it to the relationship and the amount.

### Step 1: a few days before the due date (optional)

A short, friendly heads-up avoids many delays, especially with large companies:

> "Hi Sarah, just a quick note that invoice #1042 for £1,800 is due on 15 March. Let me know if you need anything from me to process it. Thanks!"

### Step 2: 1 to 3 days after the due date: friendly reminder

Assume good faith. Attach the invoice again.

> "Hi Sarah, I hope you're well. I noticed invoice #1042 (£1,800, due 15 March) is still showing as unpaid on my side. Could you check whether it has been processed? I've attached a copy for convenience. Thanks a lot!"

### Step 3: 7 to 14 days late: firmer reminder

Be direct, ask for a date, and copy accounts payable if you can.

> "Hi Sarah, I'm following up on invoice #1042 (£1,800), now 10 days overdue. Could you let me know when payment will be made? If there is an issue with the invoice, please tell me so I can fix it quickly."

### Step 4: 15 to 30 days late: phone call

A call often unblocks things faster than emails. Ask simple questions: **has the invoice been approved, who is blocking it, what is the expected payment date?** Then confirm what was said by email the same day.

At this stage, you can also **pause ongoing work** if your contract allows it. Continuing to deliver without being paid weakens your position.

### Step 5: 30+ days late: final reminder before action

State the amount, the overdue period, a **final deadline** (often 7 or 14 days), and what you will do next.

> "Dear Ms Smith, despite my previous reminders of 16 March, 25 March and my call of 2 April, invoice #1042 for £1,800 remains unpaid. Please settle it by 20 April. After this date, I will claim statutory late payment interest and compensation and may start legal proceedings without further notice."

Send it by email and, for significant amounts, by **recorded or tracked post**.

## Your legal options (examples by country)

Rules differ by country. Here are the main frameworks for business-to-business invoices:

- **UK**: under the **Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998**, you can claim **statutory interest of 8% above the Bank of England base rate**, plus a **fixed compensation of £40** (debts under £1,000), **£70** (£1,000 to £9,999.99) or **£100** (£10,000 or more), unless your contract sets other terms. Unpaid debts can be claimed through the county court's **Money Claim Online** service.
- **European Union**: the **Late Payment Directive (2011/7/EU)** sets a default payment period of **30 days** if none is agreed, with commercial contracts generally capped at **60 days** unless expressly agreed and not grossly unfair to the creditor. It provides for interest of at least **8 percentage points above the ECB reference rate** and a minimum **€40** flat compensation for recovery costs. Each country transposes these rules into national law.
- **United States**: there is **no single federal late payment law** for private B2B invoices. Late fees and interest depend on **your contract** and state law (including usury limits). Small claims court limits vary by state.

In all cases, a clear **late payment clause in your contract or terms** makes recovery much easier. Check local rules, and for large amounts, ask a lawyer or a debt recovery service.

## Other options before going to court

- **Offer a payment plan**: splitting a large invoice into two or three payments is often better than waiting months.
- **Mediation**: some trade bodies and local business organisations offer free or low-cost mediation.
- **Debt collection agency**: they typically take a percentage of the amount recovered. Useful for clients who ignore you entirely.
- **Invoice factoring**: you sell the invoice to a third party for immediate cash, minus a fee. This helps cash flow but does not solve the relationship.

## How to get paid on time next time

- **Agree payment terms in writing** before starting work.
- Ask for a **deposit** (30 to 50% is common for freelancers) on new clients or large projects.
- **Invoice immediately** after delivery, not at the end of the month.
- Offer **easy payment methods**: bank transfer, card, payment link.
- Use invoicing software with **automatic reminders**.
- For recurring clients who pay late, shorten terms or require payment before delivery.

These habits are part of a solid freelance setup, explained in [how to start freelancing](/en/how-to-start-freelancing/). And keeping a cash buffer, like an [emergency fund](/en/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund/), means one late client does not put you in difficulty.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting weeks before the first reminder, out of fear of upsetting the client.
- Sending aggressive messages at the first delay.
- Keeping everything verbal, with no written record.
- Continuing to work for a client who has not paid several invoices.
- Threatening legal action and then not following through.

## Summary

To chase an unpaid invoice: **check your invoice**, send a **friendly reminder** right after the due date, a **firmer one** after 1 to 2 weeks, **call**, then send a **final notice with a deadline**. Keep everything in writing. Depending on your country, you may be entitled to **late payment interest and fixed compensation**. Prevent future delays with **clear terms, deposits and automatic reminders**.
