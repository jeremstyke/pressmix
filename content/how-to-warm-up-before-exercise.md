A good warm-up takes **about 5 to 15 minutes** and follows three steps: **light cardio to raise your heart rate, dynamic movements for your joints and muscles, then a few gentle reps of the activity you are about to do**. Save long, static stretches for after your workout. Here is a simple routine you can use before most sports.

## Why warm up?

Jumping straight into intense exercise is hard on the body. A warm-up:

- gradually **raises your heart rate and breathing**;
- **increases blood flow** to the muscles;
- **raises muscle temperature**, which makes them more supple;
- prepares your joints and coordination;
- helps you **focus** on the session ahead.

Warming up is widely recommended to prepare the body for effort and is often included in injury prevention programs, although no warm-up can remove every risk.

## How long should a warm-up last?

For most people, **5 to 15 minutes** is enough. Adjust it to:

- **the intensity** of the session: the harder the workout, the more gradual the warm-up should be;
- **the temperature**: you may need longer on cold days;
- **your age and fitness level**.

You should feel **warm, slightly out of breath and ready**, not tired.

## The 3-step routine

### Step 1: Light cardio (3 to 5 minutes)

Start with any low-intensity movement:

- brisk walking or easy jogging;
- cycling at a gentle pace;
- skipping with a rope or jumping jacks;
- marching on the spot with arm swings.

Increase the pace gradually. You should still be able to talk easily.

### Step 2: Dynamic movements (3 to 5 minutes)

Dynamic stretches move your joints through their range of motion without holding positions. Do each one for 30 seconds or around 10 reps:

1. **Arm circles**, forwards then backwards.
2. **Leg swings**, front to back and side to side, holding a wall if needed.
3. **Walking lunges**, with a gentle twist of the upper body.
4. **Hip circles** with hands on the hips.
5. **High knees** and **butt kicks**.
6. **Bodyweight squats**, slow and controlled.

### Step 3: Sport-specific preparation (2 to 5 minutes)

Finish with lighter versions of what you are about to do:

- **Running**: a few short, progressive accelerations.
- **Strength training**: one or two light sets of your first exercise.
- **Football, tennis or basketball**: passes, footwork and light drills with the ball.
- **Swimming**: a few easy lengths using different strokes.

## Static or dynamic stretching?

**Static stretching** means holding a position for a while. Before exercise, long static stretches are generally not recommended as the main warm-up, as they do not prepare the body for movement as well as dynamic exercises. Keep them for **after** your session or for separate flexibility work.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Skipping the warm-up** because you are short on time: shorten the session instead.
- **Going too hard too soon**: the warm-up should prepare you, not exhaust you.
- **Bouncing** into stretches.
- **Ignoring pain**: discomfort that is sharp or unusual is a reason to stop.

If you are new to running, combine this routine with our guide on [how to start running as a beginner](/en/how-to-start-running-as-a-beginner/), and remember to [drink enough water](/en/how-much-water-should-you-drink-a-day/) around your workouts. If you have a health condition or have been inactive for a long time, talk to a doctor before starting a new exercise program.

## Summary

Warm up for 5 to 15 minutes before exercise: start with light cardio, continue with dynamic movements like leg swings, lunges and arm circles, then finish with easy versions of your sport. Keep long static stretches for after your workout and adjust the warm-up to the intensity, the weather and your fitness level.
