Most dogs only need a bath **every few weeks to every few months**, or simply when they are dirty or smelly. The right frequency depends on your dog's **coat type, skin and lifestyle**. Bathing too often can strip the natural oils that protect the skin, so regular brushing usually matters more than frequent baths.

## Why you should not bathe a dog too often

A dog's skin produces **natural oils** that keep the coat shiny and the skin protected. Washing too frequently, especially with the wrong product, can lead to:

- dry, flaky skin;
- itching and scratching;
- a dull coat;
- sometimes a stronger smell, as the skin produces more oil to compensate.

## How often, depending on the coat

There is no single rule, but these general guidelines help:

- **Short, smooth coats** (such as Beagles or Boxers): they stay fairly clean, and an occasional bath is often enough.
- **Long or silky coats** (such as Yorkshire Terriers or Shih Tzus): they tend to need more regular baths and frequent brushing to avoid tangles.
- **Double coats** (such as Huskies or German Shepherds): baths are needed less often, but heavy brushing is essential, especially during shedding seasons.
- **Curly or wool-like coats** (such as Poodles): they often need regular baths and professional grooming.
- **Oily coats** (some hound and retriever types): may need more frequent washing to control odor.

## Lifestyle matters too

A dog that **swims in lakes, rolls in mud or lives outdoors** will need more baths than a small dog who mostly stays on the sofa. Likewise, a dog with a **skin condition** may need medicated baths on a schedule set by the vet.

Puppies can be bathed, but use a gentle puppy shampoo and keep them warm afterwards.

## Choose the right products

- Use a **shampoo made for dogs**. Human shampoo is not adapted to the pH of a dog's skin and can irritate it.
- For sensitive skin, choose a mild, fragrance-free formula.
- Ask your vet about medicated shampoos if your dog has itching, redness or flakes.

## How to bathe your dog step by step

1. **Brush first** to remove loose hair and knots, which are harder to remove when wet.
2. Use **lukewarm water**, never hot.
3. Wet the coat thoroughly, avoiding the eyes and ears.
4. Apply shampoo and massage it down to the skin.
5. **Rinse very well**: shampoo residue is a common cause of itching.
6. Dry with a towel, then a hair dryer on a low, cool setting if your dog tolerates it.
7. Reward your dog with praise or a treat to keep bath time positive.

## Between baths: keep your dog clean

- **Brush regularly**, from a few times a week to daily for long coats.
- Wipe muddy paws with a damp cloth after walks.
- Clean ears and trim nails as needed.
- Wash your dog's bedding regularly.

## Signs you should talk to the vet

Contact a vet if your dog has a **persistent bad smell**, constant scratching, red patches, hair loss or scabs. These can be signs of allergies, parasites or infection, and bathing alone will not solve them.

## Summary

Bathe your dog every few weeks to every few months, or when it is dirty, adjusting to its coat, skin and lifestyle. Use a dog shampoo, rinse thoroughly and brush regularly between baths. If skin problems or strong odors persist, ask your vet for advice.
