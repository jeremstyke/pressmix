If your PS5 controller won't connect, the fix that works most often is to **plug the DualSense into the console with a USB data cable and press the PS button**. If that fails, use the **reset button** in the small hole on the back of the controller (hold it for at least 5 seconds), then reconnect with the cable. Below are 8 fixes to try in order, plus what the light bar is telling you.

## What the light bar is telling you

Before trying anything, look at the controller when you press the PS button:

- **No light at all**: the battery is probably fully drained or the controller is not getting power. Start with fix 1.
- **Light bar flashing blue, then off**: the controller is looking for a console but can't find the one it is paired with. Fixes 2 and 3.
- **Flashes once, then nothing**: often a battery or firmware issue. Fixes 1, 3 and 6.
- **Connected but input lag or drops**: wireless interference or distance. Fix 5.

## Fix 1: charge it properly

A DualSense with a completely empty battery can take a few minutes before it even shows a light.

1. Plug it into the PS5 with a **USB-C cable**, on a front or rear USB port.
2. Make sure the console is on, or in **Rest Mode with USB power enabled** (Settings > System > Power Saving > Features Available in Rest Mode > Supply Power to USB Ports).
3. Wait **10 to 15 minutes**. The light bar should pulse orange while charging in Rest Mode.

If nothing happens, try another cable and another port before going further.

## Fix 2: use a real data cable

This is the number one trap. Many USB-C cables (especially those that came with power banks or cheap chargers) are **charge-only**: they supply power but cannot carry data. The controller will charge but **will never pair** through them.

- Use the cable that came with the PS5, or a cable you know transfers data (one you use to copy files from a phone, for example).
- Plug it directly into the console, not through a hub.
- Then press the **PS button**: the light bar should blink and then stay lit, and the console will show the controller's user.

## Fix 3: reset the DualSense

The reset forces the controller to forget its pairing and restart its firmware. This is the procedure described by PlayStation support:

1. **Turn off the PS5** completely (not Rest Mode).
2. Unplug the USB cable from the controller.
3. Find the **small hole on the back of the controller, next to the Sony logo**.
4. Insert a straightened paper clip or a SIM ejector pin and **press and hold the button for at least 5 seconds**.
5. Plug the controller into the PS5 with the USB cable, turn the console on and press the **PS button**.

This solves most "flashing blue then off" cases, especially after you used the controller on a PC or a phone.

## Fix 4: re-pair if you used it on another device

The DualSense remembers **only one host at a time** over Bluetooth. If you paired it with a PC, a Mac, a phone or a second PS5, it will try to reconnect there.

- Connect it to the PS5 with the cable and press PS: this re-pairs it with the console.
- To pair it wirelessly with another device later, hold **Create + PS** for about 3 seconds until the light bar flashes quickly.

## Fix 5: reduce wireless interference

If the controller connects but drops or lags:

- Sit closer to the console, ideally **within a few metres with nothing blocking the path** (cabinets, a TV in front of the PS5, metal shelves).
- Move the PS5 out of a closed TV cabinet.
- Keep **Wi-Fi routers, wireless headsets and microwaves** away from the console. They share the 2.4 GHz band with Bluetooth.
- Disconnect other Bluetooth accessories you don't use.
- If your PS5 uses Wi-Fi, try a **wired Ethernet connection**: it frees up the radio and improves online play too. Our guide on [how to fix lag in online games](/en/how-to-fix-lag-in-online-games/) covers this in detail.

## Fix 6: update the console and the controller

Sony regularly releases system software updates that include controller fixes.

1. Go to **Settings > System > System Software > System Software Update and Settings** and install any update.
2. Then go to **Settings > Accessories > Controllers (General)** and look for **Wireless Controller Device Software**. If an update is offered, install it with the controller plugged in by USB.

Do not unplug the controller during a firmware update.

## Fix 7: test the controller elsewhere

To find out whether the problem is the controller or the console:

- Try **another controller** on your PS5. If it connects fine, the problem is your DualSense.
- Try your DualSense on a **PC** (via cable or Bluetooth). If it is recognised, the controller works and the issue is the pairing with the console.
- If neither controller connects, restart the PS5 fully and rebuild nothing yet: first try **Safe Mode** option 1 (Restart PS5).

## Fix 8: battery or hardware fault

If the controller no longer lights up even after 30 minutes on a known-good cable, or disconnects as soon as you unplug it, the **battery** or the **USB-C port** may be failing.

- Check the port for **dust or lint** and clean it gently with a wooden toothpick or a burst of compressed air.
- If the controller is under warranty, contact **PlayStation Support** for a repair or replacement rather than opening it, which would void the warranty.

## Common mistakes

- Using a phone charging cable that does not carry data, then concluding the controller is dead.
- Doing the reset while the PS5 is still on and expecting it to re-pair wirelessly.
- Pressing the reset button for one second only: hold it **at least 5 seconds**.
- Leaving the controller paired to a PC and wondering why it no longer connects to the console.

> **Tip:** label your data cable with a piece of tape. When a controller acts up, you will know immediately that the cable is not the problem.

## Summary

When a PS5 controller won't connect, **charge it with a USB data cable** and press the PS button. If it still fails, **turn off the console, hold the reset button for at least 5 seconds**, then reconnect with the cable. Re-pair it if you used it on another device, reduce **2.4 GHz interference**, update the console and controller software, and test with another controller to isolate the fault. If it is still dead, contact PlayStation Support. While you are in the settings, protect your PlayStation Network account with a [strong password](/en/how-to-create-a-strong-password/) and two-step verification.
