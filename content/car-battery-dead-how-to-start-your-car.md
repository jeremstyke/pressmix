If your car battery is dead, you can start the car with **jumper cables connected to another car**, with a **portable jump starter (booster)**, or by **recharging the battery with a charger**. Once the engine runs, drive for **at least 30 minutes**, ideally on a road where you can keep a steady speed, to put charge back in. If the battery dies again within days, have the **battery and the alternator tested**: many auto parts shops do it for free.

## Is it really the battery?

Typical signs of a dead or weak battery:

- the engine **cranks slowly** ("rr... rr... rr") or not at all;
- you hear **rapid clicking** when you turn the key or press start;
- dashboard lights are **dim or flicker**, or nothing lights up;
- central locking does not respond to the remote.

On the other hand, if the dashboard lights are bright and the engine **cranks normally but does not fire**, the battery is probably fine: the problem is more likely fuel, ignition or an immobiliser issue. If nothing happens at all with bright lights, it can be the **starter motor**.

## Method 1: jump-start with jumper cables

You need a second car with a battery of the **same voltage** (12 V for almost all cars) and a set of jumper cables.

### Before you start

- Park the cars close together **without them touching**.
- Turn off both engines, lights, radio and air conditioning.
- Check the dead battery: if it is **cracked, leaking, swollen or frozen**, do not jump it. Call for assistance.
- Check your owner's manual: some cars have **dedicated jump-start terminals** under the bonnet, away from the battery.

### The connection order (very important)

1. **Red clamp** on the **positive (+)** terminal of the **dead** battery.
2. Other **red clamp** on the **positive (+)** terminal of the **good** battery.
3. **Black clamp** on the **negative (-)** terminal of the **good** battery.
4. Last **black clamp** on an **unpainted metal part** of the engine block or chassis of the **broken-down car**, away from the battery (not on the dead battery's negative terminal). This reduces the risk of a spark near battery gases.

### Starting

1. Start the **helper car** and let it run for **2 to 5 minutes**, slightly above idle.
2. Try to start the broken-down car. If it fails, wait another few minutes and try again. Do not crank for more than **10 seconds** at a time.
3. Once it starts, **remove the cables in reverse order**: black from the chassis, black from the good battery, red from the good battery, red from the dead battery. Never let the clamps touch each other.

## Method 2: use a portable jump starter

A lithium jump starter (booster) lets you start the car **alone**, without a second vehicle. It is the best thing to keep in the boot, especially in winter.

1. Check that the booster is **charged** (most have an indicator).
2. Connect the **red clamp to positive (+)**, then the **black clamp to the chassis or negative (-)**, following the booster's manual.
3. Turn the booster on, then start the car.
4. Disconnect immediately after the engine starts, then recharge the booster at home.

Choose a booster rated for your engine size: diesel and large petrol engines need more peak current than a small city car.

## Method 3: recharge the battery with a charger

If you are at home and not in a hurry, a **smart battery charger** is gentler for the battery.

1. Read the charger's instructions. With modern smart chargers, you can usually charge **without disconnecting** the battery from the car.
2. Connect red to positive, black to negative (or to the chassis, as instructed).
3. Plug in the charger and select the right mode (standard, AGM, EFB, lithium...).
4. A deeply discharged battery can take **several hours to a full night** to recharge.

## Method 4: push-starting (manual cars only)

On a **manual** car, you can push-start: second gear, clutch down, ignition on, push to around 10 km/h (6 mph), then release the clutch quickly. It is not recommended on many modern cars, especially with catalytic converters or diesel particulate filters, and **it is impossible on automatics**. Use it as a last resort only.

## After the restart: how long to drive?

- Drive for **at least 30 minutes**, ideally with some sustained speed rather than stop-and-go traffic.
- Avoid switching off the engine at the first stop.
- Limit heavy consumers (heated seats, rear demister) during the first minutes if possible.

Even then, the alternator does not always fully recharge a deeply discharged battery. A full charge with a charger is ideal afterwards.

## Why did the battery die?

- **Lights or an accessory left on**, a door not fully closed (interior light), a dashcam wired permanently.
- **Short trips only**: the alternator does not have time to recharge what starting the engine used.
- **Cold weather**: a battery loses a big part of its capacity in freezing temperatures, while the engine needs more power to start.
- **Car unused for weeks**: modern cars draw a small current permanently (alarm, electronics).
- **Age**: a car battery typically lasts **around 3 to 5 years**, depending on climate and use.
- **Charging system fault**: a failing **alternator** or a parasitic drain from a faulty component.

## How to prevent a dead battery

- If the car sits for **more than two weeks**, connect a **battery maintainer** (trickle charger) or drive it for 30 minutes weekly.
- Have the battery **tested before winter**, especially after 4 years.
- Keep terminals **clean and tight**: white or green deposits can be cleaned with a wire brush and a baking soda solution.
- Get into the habit of checking lights before leaving the car.
- Include the battery in your regular checks, along with [tire pressure](/en/how-to-check-tire-pressure/) and [oil changes](/en/how-often-should-you-change-engine-oil/).

## When to replace the battery

Replace it if:

- it fails a **load test** at a garage or parts shop;
- it dies **again** after a full charge with no obvious cause;
- it is **swollen, leaking** or the terminals are badly corroded;
- it is more than 5 years old and starting is getting slower.

Make sure the new battery has the right **technology** (standard, EFB or AGM, often required on cars with start-stop), **capacity** and **cold cranking amps (CCA)**. On some recent cars, the new battery must be **registered** with the car's computer.

## Common mistakes

- Connecting the last black clamp to the dead battery's negative terminal.
- Reversing polarity: it can damage electronics and fuses.
- Switching off the engine right after a jump-start.
- Jump-starting a battery that is frozen or damaged.

## Summary

A dead battery is fixed with **jumper cables** (red on +, red on +, black on the good battery's -, black on bare metal of the broken-down car), a **portable jump starter** or a **charger**. Drive at least **30 minutes** afterwards. If it happens again, have the **battery and alternator tested**, and replace a battery that is **old, swollen or failing tests**.
