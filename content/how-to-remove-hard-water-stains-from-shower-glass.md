To remove hard water stains from shower glass, **spray warm white vinegar (or a citric acid solution)**, **let it soak for 15 to 30 minutes** so it dissolves the limescale, scrub with a **non-scratch sponge**, rinse and **dry with a squeegee**. For thick, old buildup, use a **vinegar-soaked paper towel compress** for 1 to 2 hours or a **baking soda and vinegar paste**. Then a quick squeegee after each shower keeps the glass clear.

## Why shower glass turns cloudy

Hard water contains dissolved **calcium and magnesium**. When droplets dry on the glass, water evaporates and the minerals stay behind as white spots. Over weeks, these spots merge into a **cloudy film**, often mixed with soap scum (soap combined with those same minerals). The harder your water, the faster it builds up.

Limescale is **alkaline**, which is why **acids** (vinegar, citric acid, lemon) dissolve it. Scrubbing alone barely works and risks scratching.

## What you need

- **White vinegar** (around 5 to 8% acidity; "cleaning vinegar" is stronger and works faster);
- or **citric acid powder** (sold for descaling, often cheaper per use);
- a **spray bottle**;
- a **non-scratch sponge** or microfibre cloth;
- **paper towels** for compresses;
- **baking soda** for tough spots;
- a **squeegee** and a dry microfibre cloth;
- rubber **gloves**.

## Method 1: warm vinegar spray (light to moderate stains)

1. **Warm the vinegar** in a microwave-safe container for 30 to 60 seconds. Warm acid works noticeably faster than cold.
2. Pour it into the spray bottle. For a more pleasant smell and better cling, you can mix **2 parts vinegar with 1 part dish soap**: the soap helps it stay on vertical glass and cuts soap scum.
3. **Spray generously** over the whole glass, starting from the bottom (to avoid streaks running over dry glass).
4. **Wait 15 to 30 minutes**. Re-spray if it starts to dry.
5. **Scrub in circles** with the non-scratch sponge, insisting on the lower part where buildup is thickest.
6. **Rinse with warm water** and dry immediately with the squeegee, then a microfibre cloth.

## Method 2: citric acid (stronger, less smell)

1. Dissolve **2 tablespoons of citric acid in 500 ml (about 2 cups) of warm water**.
2. Spray on the glass and leave for **10 to 20 minutes**.
3. Scrub, rinse thoroughly and dry.

Citric acid is odourless and very effective. Rinse well: residue left on the glass can leave its own faint film.

## Method 3: compress for thick, old buildup

When the glass is white and rough to the touch:

1. Soak **paper towels in warm vinegar**.
2. Stick them flat on the glass: they adhere naturally when wet. Cover them with **plastic cling film** to slow evaporation.
3. Leave for **1 to 2 hours**, or even longer on very old deposits, re-wetting if needed.
4. Remove, then scrub with a paste of **baking soda and a little water**: it acts as a gentle abrasive. It will fizz when it meets the vinegar left on the glass, which is normal.
5. Rinse, dry, and repeat once if some spots remain.

For the **bottom rail and corners**, an old toothbrush works well.

## Method 4: when home remedies are not enough

- A **commercial limescale remover** for bathrooms (check it is suitable for glass and your fittings).
- A **melamine sponge** ("magic eraser") used gently on wet glass can remove the last film. Test on a corner first and avoid it on coated glass.
- On **very old, etched glass**, the mineral deposits may have actually damaged the surface. In that case, no cleaner will make it perfectly clear: the cloudiness is in the glass itself.

## What NOT to use

- **Abrasive scouring pads** or steel wool: they scratch the glass permanently.
- **Razor blades** on coated glass: many modern shower doors have an **anti-limescale coating** that a blade or harsh powder will remove.
- **Bleach mixed with vinegar**: this mixture releases **toxic chlorine gas**. Never combine them.
- Leaving vinegar for hours on **natural stone** (marble, limestone) around the shower: acids etch stone. Protect it with a towel.
- Long vinegar exposure on **seals and some metal finishes**: rinse fittings well.

## Keep the glass clear: the 30-second routine

Removing limescale once is the hard part. Keeping it away is easy:

1. **Squeegee the glass after every shower**, top to bottom. It takes about 30 seconds and removes most of the water before it can dry.
2. Leave the door or screen **open** to let the bathroom dry, and run the extractor fan. A dry room also limits mould: see [how to reduce humidity in a house](/en/how-to-reduce-humidity-in-a-house/).
3. **Once a week**, spray diluted vinegar (half vinegar, half water), leave a few minutes, rinse and squeegee.
4. Consider a **water-repellent glass treatment**: it makes water bead and run off, and makes weekly cleaning much quicker.

> **Tip:** keep the squeegee hanging inside the shower. If it is within reach, you will actually use it.

## Special cases

- **Very hard water areas**: a **water softener** or a shower filter reduces deposits everywhere (glass, taps, kettle). Your kettle will thank you too: see [how to descale a kettle](/en/how-to-descale-a-kettle/).
- **Frosted or textured glass**: limescale hides in the texture. Use a soft brush instead of a sponge.
- **Shower screens with metal frames**: clean the frame with diluted vinegar, then rinse to protect the finish.

## FAQ

### How long should vinegar sit on shower glass?

From **15 to 30 minutes** for normal stains, up to **1 to 2 hours** under a compress for thick buildup.

### Does lemon juice work?

Yes, it contains citric acid, but it is weaker and more expensive than citric acid powder. It is fine for small spots.

### Why does my glass look streaky after cleaning?

Usually from **drying in the air** or residue. Rinse thoroughly and finish with a squeegee and a dry microfibre cloth.

## Summary

Hard water stains are **mineral deposits** that acids dissolve. Spray **warm vinegar or citric acid**, wait **15 to 30 minutes**, scrub gently, rinse and squeegee. For old buildup, use a **vinegar compress** for 1 to 2 hours and a **baking soda paste**. Never mix bleach with vinegar and avoid abrasive pads. After that, a **30-second squeegee after each shower** keeps the glass clear.
