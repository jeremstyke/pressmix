To build an emergency fund, **set a target of three to six months of essential expenses**, open a separate savings account and **save a fixed amount automatically every payday**. Start small: even a first goal of one month of expenses already protects you from most everyday surprises.

## What is an emergency fund?

An emergency fund is money set aside **only for unexpected and necessary expenses**: a car repair, a broken boiler, a medical bill or a sudden loss of income. It is not for holidays, gifts or a new sofa.

Its job is simple: **avoid going into debt** when life gets in the way. Without it, a single bad surprise can end up on a credit card with high interest.

## How much should you save?

The most common guideline is **three to six months of essential expenses**. Note the word "essential": you count what you truly need to live, not your whole spending.

1. Add up your monthly essentials: housing, utilities, food, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments.
2. Multiply by three for a basic safety net, or by six for a more comfortable one.

Aim for the **higher end** if:

- your income is irregular (freelancer, commission, seasonal work);
- you are the only earner in your household;
- you have children or other dependents;
- your job is in a sector with frequent layoffs.

## Step 1: Start with a mini goal

Three months of expenses can feel out of reach. Break it down. A **first goal of one month** of essentials, or even a smaller round amount, is motivating and already covers many common repairs. Once you reach it, move on to the next level.

## Step 2: Open a separate account

Keep your emergency fund **away from your current account**, otherwise you will spend it without noticing. The ideal account is:

- **easy to access** within a day or two;
- **safe**, with no risk of losing value (not invested in stocks or crypto);
- **paying some interest**, such as a high-yield or instant-access savings account.

Check that the account is covered by your country's deposit protection scheme.

## Step 3: Automate your savings

The most effective method is to **pay yourself first**. Set up an automatic transfer to your emergency account the day after your salary arrives. Even a small amount adds up month after month, and you will not miss what you never see.

This fits naturally into a [monthly budget](/en/how-to-make-a-monthly-budget/), where savings are planned like any other bill.

### Find extra money

To speed things up, look for quick wins:

- **cancel unused subscriptions**;
- sell items you no longer use;
- save tax refunds, bonuses and cash gifts;
- cut one regular expense, such as takeaway meals, for a few months.

Even small cuts make a difference over a year.

## When should you use it?

Before dipping into the fund, ask yourself three questions:

1. **Is it unexpected?**
2. **Is it necessary?**
3. **Is it urgent?**

If the answer is yes to all three, use it without guilt: that is what it is for. Then **rebuild it** as soon as possible by restarting your automatic transfers.

### Common mistakes to avoid

- **Investing the fund**: markets can fall exactly when you need the money.
- **Using it for planned expenses**: holidays and Christmas need their own savings pot.
- **Waiting to have "enough" to start**: starting small beats not starting.
- **Forgetting to update it**: if your rent or family grows, your target should too.

If your income is irregular, as it often is when you [start freelancing](/en/how-to-start-freelancing/), aim for the higher target. If you are unsure how to balance saving with paying off debt, a financial advisor can help you build a plan suited to your situation.

## Summary

An emergency fund covers three to six months of essential expenses and is kept in a separate, safe and accessible account. Start with a small goal, automate a transfer every payday and only use the money for real emergencies. Once it is in place, you will handle surprises calmly instead of reaching for a credit card.
