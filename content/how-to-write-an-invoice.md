To write an invoice, include **your details and your client's, a unique invoice number, the date, a clear description of the work, the amount due, any taxes and your payment terms**. Send it promptly as a PDF and keep a copy. The exact legal requirements depend on your country, so check them before sending your first one.

## What is an invoice?

An invoice is a document that **asks a client to pay** for goods or services you have provided. It also serves as an **official record** for your accounts and taxes, both for you and for your client. A clear invoice helps you get paid faster and avoids disputes.

## What to include on an invoice

Most invoices contain the following information:

- **The word "Invoice"** clearly at the top.
- **Your details**: name or business name, address, contact details and, if applicable, your business registration or tax number.
- **Your client's details**: company name, address and contact person.
- **A unique invoice number**, following a logical sequence.
- **The invoice date** and, if relevant, the date the work was done or delivered.
- **A description of the goods or services**: quantity, unit price and total for each line.
- **The subtotal, taxes and total amount due**.
- **Payment terms**: due date, accepted payment methods and your bank details.

In many countries, other mentions are required, for example a VAT number, a tax exemption mention or late payment conditions. Your tax authority's website or an accountant can tell you exactly what applies.

## How to write an invoice step by step

1. **Choose a tool**: a spreadsheet or word-processor template, or invoicing software. Software can number invoices automatically and track payments.
2. **Add your business details** and logo if you have one.
3. **Assign the next invoice number** in your sequence. Never reuse a number or skip around without reason.
4. **Fill in the client's details** exactly as they appear in their records. Ask whether they need a purchase order number.
5. **List each item** with a clear description, quantity, rate and line total.
6. **Calculate taxes** if you are required to charge them.
7. **Write your payment terms** and due date.
8. **Proofread**, save as a PDF and send it to the right person, often the accounts department.

## Setting clear payment terms

Agree on payment terms **before starting the work**, ideally in your contract, then repeat them on the invoice:

- the **due date** (for example, a set number of days after the invoice date);
- the **payment methods** you accept;
- whether you require a **deposit** for large projects;
- what happens if payment is late, within what the law allows in your country.

Clear terms reduce misunderstandings and late payments.

## How to get paid on time

- **Invoice immediately** when the work is delivered or at the agreed milestone.
- Make paying easy with clear bank details or a payment link.
- **Send a polite reminder** shortly after the due date passes, then follow up firmly if needed.
- Keep track of which invoices are paid and which are outstanding.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Missing or **duplicate invoice numbers**.
- **Vague descriptions** such as "services" with no detail.
- Forgetting the **due date** or bank details.
- Sending the invoice to the wrong contact.
- Not keeping copies: invoices must usually be stored for several years, as explained in our guide on [how long to keep important documents](/en/how-long-to-keep-important-documents/).

If you are just getting started, read [how to start freelancing](/en/how-to-start-freelancing/), and keep an [emergency fund](/en/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund/) for months when clients pay late.

## Summary

A good invoice includes your details and your client's, a unique number, the date, a clear description of the work, the total with taxes and your payment terms. Check your country's legal requirements, send invoices promptly as a PDF, follow up on late payments and keep copies for your records.
