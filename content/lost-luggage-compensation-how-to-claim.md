If an airline loses or delays your checked bag, **file a report at the airport before leaving the baggage hall** (a Property Irregularity Report, PIR), keep **all receipts** for essentials you buy, and send a **written claim** to the airline within the deadlines: **21 days** after receiving a delayed bag, or as soon as the bag is declared lost (usually after **21 days** of searching). On international flights covered by the **Montreal Convention**, the airline's liability is capped at **1,519 Special Drawing Rights (SDR)** per passenger, roughly US$2,000, since 28 December 2024.

## Step 1: report it at the airport, immediately

Do not leave the airport without reporting the problem.

1. Go to the **baggage service desk** of the airline (or its handling agent) in the arrivals hall.
2. Fill in a **Property Irregularity Report (PIR)**. You will need your **boarding pass**, your **baggage tag receipt** (the sticker usually stuck on your boarding pass or passport) and a description of the bag (brand, colour, size, distinguishing features).
3. Give a **delivery address** and phone number.
4. **Keep the file reference number**: it lets you track the search online, usually through the WorldTracer system used by many airlines.
5. Ask about the airline's policy for **interim expenses** (toiletries, clothes) and whether they provide an overnight kit.

If you discover damage or missing items only after leaving, report it as soon as possible in writing.

## The deadlines you must respect

Under the **Montreal Convention**, which applies to most international flights:

- **Damaged baggage**: written complaint within **7 days** of receiving it.
- **Delayed baggage**: written complaint within **21 days** from the date the bag was delivered to you.
- **Lost baggage**: if the airline admits the loss, or if the bag has not arrived **21 days** after the date it should have, you can claim it as lost.
- **Legal action**: must be brought within **2 years**.

Airlines' own conditions may set similar or shorter internal deadlines for claims, so **file early**.

## How much compensation can you get?

### Montreal Convention limits

The Montreal Convention caps the airline's liability for **destruction, loss, damage or delay of baggage at 1,519 SDR** per passenger. This limit was raised from 1,288 SDR on **28 December 2024**, following ICAO's regular inflation review. The SDR is a basket currency defined by the IMF, and its value moves daily: check its current value against your currency when you make the claim.

Important: this is a **maximum, not a fixed payout**. You must **prove your loss**: the airline reimburses the actual value of what was lost, usually **minus depreciation** for older items, not the price of new ones.

### Delayed bags: interim expenses

For a delayed bag, you can claim **reasonable expenses** for essentials: toiletries, underwear, a change of clothes, a phone charger. Keep it proportionate. A new designer suit for a two-day delay will likely be refused. Keep **every receipt**.

### Refund of baggage fees

- In the **United States**, under DOT rules adopted in 2024, airlines must **refund the checked bag fee** when you have filed a mishandled baggage report and the bag is not delivered within **12 hours** of a domestic flight's arrival at the gate, or **15 to 30 hours** for international flights, depending on flight length.
- Elsewhere, ask for the bag fee back as part of your claim if the bag was lost: you paid for a service that was not delivered.

### Declaring higher value

If you travel with valuable items in checked baggage, you can sometimes buy a **special declaration of interest** (excess valuation) at check-in to raise the airline's liability. In practice, the better option is to keep **valuables, electronics, medication and documents in your cabin bag**: see [what to pack in a carry-on bag](/en/what-to-pack-in-a-carry-on-bag/).

## Step 2: write your claim

Send the claim through the airline's online form, or by email and tracked letter. Include:

- your **PIR reference**, booking reference, flight number and date;
- your **boarding pass and baggage tag**;
- a **list of contents** with estimated value, age and, when possible, proof of purchase (receipts, bank statements, photos of you with the items);
- **receipts** for interim purchases;
- your **bank details** for payment.

Keep a copy of everything. If the airline does not answer within a few weeks, send a reminder. Our guide on [how to write a complaint letter](/en/how-to-write-a-complaint-letter/) gives a structure you can adapt.

## Example of a claim

Sophie flies from London to Montreal. Her bag arrives **4 days late**. She files a PIR at the airport and buys:

- toiletries: £25;
- underwear and two T-shirts: £60;
- a phone charger: £15.

Total: **£100**, all with receipts. She sends her claim with the PIR number within 21 days of receiving her bag. This is a reasonable, well-documented claim that airlines generally pay.

If her bag had been **lost**, she would have listed the contents with values and proofs, and the airline would have paid the depreciated value, up to the 1,519 SDR cap.

## Which airline should you claim from?

- On a **single booking with connections**, you can generally claim from the **last carrier** that operated your flight, which is usually the one at your destination's baggage desk. Under the Montreal Convention, you can also claim against the first carrier or the one on which the loss occurred.
- On **separate tickets**, it is more complicated: start with the airline you checked the bag with last.

## If the airline refuses or does not pay

1. Check that you met the deadlines and sent all proofs.
2. Escalate to the airline's **customer relations** in writing.
3. Contact your **travel insurance** or **credit card insurance**: many cover delayed or lost baggage, sometimes on top of the airline.
4. Use an **alternative dispute resolution** body or national aviation authority where available (for example, approved ADR schemes in the UK, the national enforcement body in EU countries, or a complaint to the US Department of Transportation).
5. As a last resort, **small claims court**, within the 2-year limit.

## Tips to avoid problems

- Put an **AirTag or other tracker** in your bag: it helps you tell the airline exactly where it is.
- Remove **old baggage tags** from your suitcase.
- Put your **name and phone number** inside and outside the bag.
- Take a **photo of your bag and its contents** before departure.
- Allow enough connection time: tight connections are a major cause of delayed bags.

> **Tip:** after a long-haul trip with no bag, getting some rest helps you deal with the paperwork calmly: see [how to get over jet lag](/en/how-to-get-over-jet-lag/).

## Summary

For a lost or delayed bag, **file a PIR at the airport**, keep **all receipts**, and send a **written claim** within **7 days** (damage) or **21 days** (delay). On international flights, the Montreal Convention caps compensation at **1,519 SDR** per passenger, based on the **actual, depreciated value** of your loss. In the US, you can also get your **bag fee refunded** for significant delays. Travel insurance and card insurance can cover the rest.
