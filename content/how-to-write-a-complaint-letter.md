To write an effective complaint letter, **stay calm and factual, explain clearly what happened, include your evidence and say exactly what you want** (a refund, a repair, a replacement), with a reasonable deadline. Send it to the right department in a way that leaves a record, and keep a copy. Here is a simple structure you can follow.

## Before you write

A strong complaint is prepared. First:

1. **Contact customer service** by phone or chat if you have not already: many problems are solved quickly that way. Note the date, time and the name of the person you spoke with.
2. **Gather your evidence**: receipts, order confirmations, contracts, photos of the problem, previous emails.
3. **Check the terms and conditions** and the warranty that apply to your purchase.
4. **Decide what you want**: a refund, a repair, a replacement, a credit or an apology.

Consumer protection laws vary by country. Your national consumer agency's website is a good place to check your rights.

## The structure of a complaint letter

### 1. Your details and reference numbers

Put your name, address and contact details at the top, then the date. Include any **order number, account number or customer reference**. This helps the company find your file immediately.

### 2. A clear subject line

For example: "Complaint about a faulty washing machine, order no. ...". The reader should understand the topic at a glance.

### 3. What happened

Describe the facts **in chronological order**: what you bought or used, when, what went wrong and what you have already done to solve it. Keep it short and precise.

### 4. What you want

State your request clearly: "I am asking for a full refund of ..." or "I request a replacement of the product". If relevant, mention your rights under the warranty or the applicable consumer law, without inventing legal references.

### 5. A deadline

Give a **reasonable deadline** for a response, such as a couple of weeks, and say what you will do if you do not hear back (for example, contacting a consumer body or an ombudsman).

### 6. Attachments and closing

List the **copies** of documents you attach (never send originals) and end politely.

## Tone: firm but polite

Anger is understandable, but an aggressive letter rarely helps. The person reading it is often not responsible for the problem. A **calm, factual and polite** tone makes you more credible and makes it easier for someone to help you.

Avoid exaggerations, insults and threats you do not intend to carry out.

## How to send it

- **By email or through the company's complaint form**: quick, and you keep a record.
- **By recorded or tracked mail**: useful for important disputes, because it proves the date of sending and receipt.

Keep copies of everything you send and receive, organized in one folder. Our guide on [how long to keep important documents](/en/how-long-to-keep-important-documents/) can help you decide how long to keep them.

## What if you get no answer?

1. **Send a reminder** referring to your first letter.
2. **Escalate** to a manager or the company's complaints department.
3. Contact an **ombudsman, mediation service or consumer organization**, depending on your country and sector.
4. For larger amounts or complex disputes, consider **legal advice**.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Writing several pages of emotional explanations.
- Forgetting your order number or contact details.
- Not saying clearly what you want.
- Sending original documents.

If you are writing a letter for a job rather than a complaint, see [how to write a cover letter](/en/how-to-write-a-cover-letter/). And if the problem involves a payment you made as a business, an accurate [invoice](/en/how-to-write-an-invoice/) is your best evidence.

## Summary

A good complaint letter is calm, factual and short: give your references, explain what happened in order, state exactly what you want, set a reasonable deadline and attach copies of your evidence. Send it in a way that leaves a record, keep copies and escalate to a mediator or consumer body if nobody replies.
