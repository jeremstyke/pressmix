To descale a kettle, **fill it halfway with equal parts white vinegar and water**, bring it to the boil, leave it to soak, then empty it and **boil clean water once or twice** to remove any taste. Lemon juice or citric acid work just as well and smell nicer. Here is the full method and how to keep limescale away.

## Why does a kettle get limescale?

Tap water contains minerals, mainly **calcium and magnesium**. When water is heated, part of these minerals turns into a hard white or beige deposit: limescale. The harder your water, the faster it builds up.

Limescale is not dangerous to health, but it has drawbacks:

- the kettle **takes longer to boil** and uses more energy;
- flakes can end up in your tea or coffee;
- the heating element can wear out sooner.

## Method 1: White vinegar

This is the classic, cheap and effective solution.

1. Fill the kettle **halfway** with a mix of half white vinegar and half water.
2. Bring it to the boil, then switch it off.
3. Leave it to soak for **about an hour**, longer if the scale is thick.
4. Empty it and rinse well.
5. Fill with clean water, boil and pour away. **Repeat once or twice** to get rid of the vinegar smell.

For heavy deposits, gently rub the inside with a soft sponge after soaking. Never use a metal scourer, which can scratch the surface.

## Method 2: Lemon juice

A good option if you dislike the smell of vinegar.

1. Squeeze one or two lemons, or cut a lemon into slices.
2. Put the juice or slices in the kettle with water up to about half full.
3. Bring to the boil and leave to soak for an hour.
4. Empty, rinse, then boil clean water once before use.

Lemon works well on light to medium limescale. For thick layers, repeat the operation or use citric acid.

## Method 3: Citric acid

Citric acid is sold as a **white powder** in supermarkets, hardware stores and online. It is often the most effective natural option and has almost no smell.

1. Dissolve one or two tablespoons of citric acid in water, enough to half fill the kettle.
2. Bring to the boil and leave to act for 15 to 30 minutes.
3. Empty, rinse and boil clean water once.

Always follow the dosage on the packet if it gives one.

### What about baking soda?

Baking soda is great for many cleaning jobs, but it is **alkaline**, while limescale dissolves in **acids**. It is therefore less effective for descaling. Keep it for cleaning the outside of the kettle or removing stains.

## How often should you descale?

It depends on your water:

- **Hard water**: about once a month.
- **Soft water**: every few months is often enough.

A simple rule: **descale as soon as you see a white film** at the bottom. Light deposits come off much more easily than an old, thick crust.

## Tips to slow limescale down

- **Empty the kettle after each use** instead of leaving water sitting in it.
- Only boil the amount of water you need: it saves time and energy.
- Use filtered water if your area has very hard water.
- Rinse the kettle regularly.

Reducing how much water you boil is also a small way to cut your energy bills, which helps if you follow a [monthly budget](/en/how-to-make-a-monthly-budget/).

### A note on safety

Always **unplug the kettle** before cleaning it, never put the base in water and let it cool before rubbing the inside. Check the manufacturer's instructions: some brands recommend a specific descaler.

## Summary

To descale a kettle, boil a mix of white vinegar and water, lemon juice or dissolved citric acid, leave it to soak, then rinse and boil clean water to remove any taste. Repeat as soon as a white film appears and empty the kettle after each use to slow limescale down.
