To fix split mayonnaise, put **a fresh egg yolk** (or **1 teaspoon of mustard**, or **1 tablespoon of warm water**) in a clean bowl, then **whisk the broken mayo back in very slowly**, a teaspoon at a time at first, then in a thin stream once it thickens. The emulsion re-forms in a few minutes and the result is as good as a mayo that never split. The key, as always, is **adding the oil slowly**.

## Why mayonnaise splits

Mayonnaise is an **emulsion**: tiny droplets of oil suspended in a small amount of water (from the yolk, lemon juice or vinegar), held together by **emulsifiers**, mainly the **lecithin** and proteins in egg yolk, plus mustard. It breaks when the droplets merge and separate from the liquid. You see it immediately: the sauce turns **greasy, grainy, runny**, with oil pooling on top.

The most common causes:

- **Oil added too fast**, especially at the start, before the emulsion has formed.
- **Too much oil for one yolk**: one large yolk can handle a lot of oil in theory, but in home kitchens, keep it to around **200 to 250 ml (about 1 cup)** per yolk to stay safe.
- **Ingredients at very different temperatures**: a yolk straight from the fridge with room-temperature oil, or a cold bowl.
- **Not enough liquid**: a very thick mayo that keeps receiving oil can "overload" and split.
- **Overheating** with an immersion blender run for too long, or storage in a warm place.

## Method 1: a new egg yolk (the most reliable)

1. Put **1 fresh egg yolk** in a clean, dry bowl, ideally at room temperature. Add a pinch of salt and, if you like, half a teaspoon of mustard.
2. Whisk it for a few seconds.
3. Add the split mayonnaise **one teaspoon at a time**, whisking vigorously between additions.
4. After 4 or 5 spoonfuls, the mixture thickens and turns glossy: the emulsion has formed again.
5. Then add the rest of the broken mayo **in a thin stream**, whisking constantly.

You end up with a slightly larger quantity of mayonnaise. If it becomes too thick, loosen it with a few drops of water or lemon juice.

## Method 2: mustard

1. Put **1 teaspoon of Dijon mustard** in a clean bowl.
2. Add the split mayo very slowly, as above, whisking hard.

Mustard contains its own emulsifiers. This method works well for mayos that have only just started to split, and changes the taste a little (more mustardy).

## Method 3: a spoon of water

When mayo splits because it became **too thick**, it is short of water, not of egg.

1. Put **1 tablespoon of warm (not hot) water** in a clean bowl.
2. Whisk in the split mayo slowly, a little at a time.

This is the quickest fix if you have no extra egg. Some cooks use a tablespoon of **cold water** instead, mainly when the mayo is warm and greasy: both work if you go slowly.

## Method 4: the immersion blender rescue

If you use a stick blender:

1. Put a fresh yolk (or a tablespoon of water and a teaspoon of mustard) in the **tall, narrow beaker** that comes with the blender.
2. Pour the broken mayo on top.
3. Put the blender head **flat at the bottom**, start it and do not move it for 10 to 15 seconds, until you see white mayo forming underneath.
4. Then slowly raise the blender to incorporate the rest.

Fast and spectacular, but stop as soon as it is thick: running the blender too long heats the sauce.

## The foolproof mayonnaise method

To avoid needing a rescue:

1. Take the egg out of the fridge **30 minutes before**, or put the yolk in a bowl over warm water for a minute.
2. Put in a bowl: **1 yolk, 1 teaspoon of mustard, a pinch of salt**. Some cooks also add a teaspoon of vinegar or lemon juice at the start: it gives the emulsion a bit more liquid.
3. Stabilise the bowl on a damp cloth so it does not spin.
4. Add the oil **drop by drop** for the first 2 to 3 tablespoons, whisking constantly. This is the critical stage.
5. Once the mix is thick and pale, add the oil **in a thin stream**.
6. If it becomes very thick, add a few drops of water or lemon juice, then continue.
7. Season at the end: salt, pepper, lemon juice or vinegar.

### Which oil?

- **Neutral oils** (sunflower, rapeseed/canola, grapeseed) give a classic, mild mayo.
- **Extra virgin olive oil alone** can taste bitter, especially with a blender, which breaks down its polyphenols. Use a mix: **3/4 neutral oil, 1/4 olive oil**.

## Safety and storage

Homemade mayonnaise uses **raw egg yolk**, which carries a small risk of **salmonella**. To reduce it:

- use **very fresh eggs**, with clean, uncracked shells;
- consider **pasteurised eggs**, especially for **pregnant women, young children, older adults and people with weakened immunity**;
- keep the mayo in the **fridge** in a closed jar and eat it **within 24 hours** or so, as most food safety advice recommends;
- never leave it at room temperature for hours, especially in summer or at a picnic.

Our guide on [how long eggs last](/en/how-long-do-eggs-last/) explains how to check egg freshness.

## Other sauces that split

The same logic applies to other emulsions:

- **Aioli**: same fixes as mayo.
- **Hollandaise and béarnaise** (warm emulsions): whisk a tablespoon of **cold water** in a clean bowl and add the split sauce slowly, or start again with a new yolk over gentle heat.
- **Vinaigrette**: it naturally separates. A teaspoon of mustard and a good shake bring it back together temporarily.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to fix the mayo by whisking harder in the same bowl: it rarely works without a new base.
- Pouring the whole split mayo onto the new yolk at once.
- Using a wet or greasy bowl.
- Adding salt or lemon too late in a very thick mayo and thinking it split: sometimes it just needs liquid.

> **Tip:** if you make mayo often, an immersion blender in a narrow jar with a whole egg is the fastest method: everything in the jar, blender at the bottom, and it is done in under a minute.

## Summary

Split mayonnaise is easy to save: start a **new base** (egg yolk, mustard or a spoon of water) in a **clean bowl** and add the broken mayo **very slowly**, then in a thin stream once it thickens. To avoid it, use ingredients at **room temperature** and add the oil **drop by drop at the start**. Keep homemade mayo in the fridge and eat it quickly.
