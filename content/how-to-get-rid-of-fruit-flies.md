To get rid of fruit flies, **remove what they feed and breed on**, such as overripe fruit, damp rubbish and dirty drains, then **trap the adults with a cider vinegar and dish soap trap**. Traps alone will not solve the problem if the source stays in the kitchen, so cleaning is the most important step.

## Why do fruit flies appear?

Fruit flies are attracted to **fermenting food and sugary liquids**. They often arrive on fruit and vegetables bought at the store or come in from outside, especially in warm weather. Females lay their eggs on ripening produce and other moist, organic matter, and new flies appear quickly, which is why a few flies can turn into many within days.

Their favorite spots:

- overripe or damaged fruit and vegetables;
- the kitchen bin and compost caddy;
- sink drains and the area around the plughole;
- empty bottles and cans of juice, beer or wine;
- damp sponges, cloths and mops.

## Step 1: Remove the source

This is the step that makes the difference.

1. **Check your fruit bowl**: throw away or eat overripe fruit. Store ripe fruit in the fridge for a while.
2. **Empty the bin** and compost caddy often, and keep them closed.
3. **Rinse bottles and cans** before recycling them.
4. **Wipe up spills** of juice, wine or syrup immediately.
5. Replace or wash **sponges and cloths**.
6. Look under appliances and in cupboards for a forgotten potato or onion that has gone bad.

## Step 2: Clean the drains

Drains can collect a film of organic matter. Clean the plughole and the area around it with a brush and hot soapy water. Some people also pour boiling water down metal drains, but check that your pipes can handle it, as **very hot water can damage some plastic pipes**.

## Step 3: Make a vinegar trap

The classic homemade trap is simple and effective.

1. Pour some **apple cider vinegar** into a small glass or jar.
2. Add **a drop of dish soap**: it breaks the surface tension so the flies sink instead of landing on the liquid.
3. Leave it uncovered, or cover with plastic wrap and poke a few small holes in it.
4. Place it **near where you see the flies**.
5. Empty and refill every few days.

### Other bait ideas

- A little **leftover wine or beer** works too.
- A piece of **very ripe banana** in a jar with a paper funnel: flies get in but struggle to find the way out.

Commercial fruit fly traps are also available if you prefer.

## Step 4: Be patient

Even after you remove the source, a few flies may keep appearing for some time as eggs already laid finish developing. Keep the kitchen clean and the traps in place, and their numbers should fall steadily.

## How to keep them from coming back

- **Wash fruit and vegetables** when you get home.
- Store ripe produce in the fridge.
- Take out the rubbish regularly, especially in summer.
- Keep **drains and sinks clean**.
- Use window screens in warm months.

## When it is not fruit flies

If small flies hover around **house plants**, they may be fungus gnats, which breed in damp potting soil. Letting the soil dry out between waterings usually helps. If you see insects you cannot identify or an infestation that keeps returning, contact a pest control professional.

## Summary

Get rid of fruit flies by removing overripe fruit, emptying bins, rinsing bottles and cleaning drains, then trap the remaining adults with apple cider vinegar and a drop of dish soap. Stay patient for a few days and keep good kitchen habits to stop them from coming back.
