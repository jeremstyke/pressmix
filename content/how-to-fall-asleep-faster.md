To fall asleep faster, **keep the same wake-up time every day**, make your bedroom **cool, dark and quiet**, put screens away before bed and use a simple relaxation technique such as slow breathing. If you cannot sleep after a while, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy. Here is how to put this into practice.

## Why can't I fall asleep?

Trouble falling asleep usually has several causes:

- **an irregular schedule**, which confuses your body clock;
- **stress and racing thoughts** at bedtime;
- **caffeine, alcohol or a heavy meal** too late in the day;
- **bright light from screens** in the evening;
- a bedroom that is too warm, noisy or bright.

The good news: most of these can be changed.

## Build a steady rhythm

### 1. Wake up at the same time every day

Your internal clock relies on regularity. **A fixed wake-up time**, even at weekends, makes falling asleep easier at night.

### 2. Get daylight in the morning

Natural light early in the day helps set your body clock. A short walk outside or breakfast by a window makes a difference.

### 3. Go to bed when you feel sleepy

Lying in bed wide awake teaches your brain that bed is for staying awake. Wait for heavy eyes and yawning.

## Prepare your evening

### 4. Watch caffeine and alcohol

Caffeine can stay in your body for many hours, so **avoid coffee, energy drinks and strong tea from the afternoon onwards**. Alcohol may make you drowsy but tends to disturb sleep later in the night.

### 5. Eat dinner a bit earlier

A large, rich meal just before bed can make it harder to settle. Prefer a lighter dinner, eaten a few hours before bedtime.

### 6. Put screens away

Phones and tablets keep your mind busy and expose you to bright light. Try to **stop using them 30 to 60 minutes before bed** and leave the phone outside the bedroom if you can.

## Make your bedroom sleep friendly

### 7. Cool, dark and quiet

- Keep the room **slightly cool**.
- Use blackout curtains or a sleep mask.
- Try earplugs or a fan if noise bothers you.

### 8. Keep the bed for sleep

Avoid working, scrolling or watching series in bed. Your brain will link the bed with rest.

## Calm your mind

### 9. Try a breathing technique

Slow breathing helps your body relax. A simple version:

1. Breathe in gently through your nose for a count of four.
2. Hold for a moment.
3. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for a longer count, around six to eight.
4. Repeat for a few minutes.

Body scans, where you relax each muscle group in turn from your toes to your head, also work well.

### 10. Write down your worries

If thoughts keep spinning, **write them on paper** an hour before bed along with tomorrow's to-do list. Your brain can let go because the information is safe.

## What if you still cannot sleep?

If you have been awake in bed for a long time, **get up**, go to another room with dim light and do something calm, such as reading a paper book. Go back to bed when you feel sleepy. Avoid checking the clock again and again.

Regular exercise also helps: even [a beginner running routine](/en/how-to-start-running-as-a-beginner/) can improve sleep, as long as you do not train hard just before bed. Staying [well hydrated during the day](/en/how-much-water-should-you-drink-a-day/) rather than drinking a lot late in the evening can also limit night-time trips to the bathroom.

## When to see a doctor

Talk to a healthcare professional if trouble sleeping lasts several weeks, affects your daytime life, or comes with loud snoring, breathing pauses or low mood. Do not start sleeping pills or supplements without medical advice.

## Summary

To fall asleep faster, keep a fixed wake-up time, get morning light, limit caffeine, screens and late heavy meals, and keep your bedroom cool, dark and quiet. Use slow breathing or a body scan to relax, and get up if sleep does not come. If the problem persists, see a doctor.
