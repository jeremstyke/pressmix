If you lose your passport abroad, **report it to the local police**, then **contact your country's nearest embassy or consulate** as soon as possible. Depending on your nationality and your travel dates, they will issue either an **emergency travel document** (valid for a single trip home) or a **temporary or emergency passport**. Bring **ID, passport photos, proof of travel** and, if you have it, a **copy of the lost passport**. Also report the passport as lost or stolen so it is cancelled and cannot be misused.

## Step 1: make sure it is really lost

Before starting the procedure, take 30 minutes to check:

- your **hotel room safe**, bags, coat pockets;
- the **reception desk**: hotels often keep passports after check-in to register guests;
- the last places you visited: restaurants, taxis (use the ride-hailing app to contact the driver), car rental office.

Be careful: in many countries, once you **report the passport as lost or stolen, it is cancelled permanently**, even if you find it later. You cannot travel with it after that.

## Step 2: report it to the local police

Go to a police station and ask for a **written report** (police report or certificate of loss/theft).

- Many embassies **require or strongly recommend** this report, and your **travel insurance** will need it for any claim.
- If you don't speak the language, ask your hotel for help or note the key words in advance.
- If your passport was stolen with your wallet, report **all stolen items** (cards, phone) on the same report.

## Step 3: contact your embassy or consulate

Find your country's nearest embassy or consulate on your foreign ministry's website. Call or email them, or follow their online process, to explain the situation and **book an appointment**. Some examples of how it works:

- **UK citizens**: you report the passport lost or stolen online and apply for an **emergency travel document (ETD)** on GOV.UK. Since 9 April 2025, the ETD costs **£125**, including courier delivery. It lets you travel to the UK, sometimes via a limited number of countries. If you are not travelling home soon, you may instead apply for a full replacement passport from abroad.
- **US citizens**: you report the loss using **Form DS-64** and usually apply in person at a US embassy or consulate with **Form DS-11**. The embassy can issue a **limited-validity passport** for urgent travel, which you can later replace with a full-validity one.
- **EU citizens**: if your own country has no embassy or consulate where you are, you are entitled to help from **any other EU country's embassy**, which can issue an **EU Emergency Travel Document (EU ETD)** for your journey home.
- **Canada, Australia and others** have similar emergency or temporary passports: check your government's travel advice website.

Embassies often have **out-of-hours emergency lines**, but standard processing generally happens during office hours. Count on **one to several working days**, longer around weekends and public holidays.

## Step 4: gather the documents

Requirements vary by country, but you will usually be asked for:

- the **police report**;
- **passport photos** that meet your country's specifications (many embassies give a list of nearby photo shops);
- **proof of identity**: national ID card, driving licence, birth certificate, or a **photocopy or photo of the lost passport**;
- **proof of travel**: your return ticket or booking;
- the **application forms**, often available online to fill in in advance;
- the **fee**, by card or in cash depending on the embassy.

> **Tip:** before every trip, take a **photo of your passport's ID page** and store it in a secure place you can access from any phone (encrypted cloud storage or email to yourself). It saves hours at the embassy. Our guide on [how long to keep important documents](/en/how-long-to-keep-important-documents/) has more on keeping copies safely.

## Step 5: sort out visas, entry stamps and your flight

- If you entered the country with a **visa** or an **entry stamp** in the lost passport, you may need to contact the local **immigration office** to get an exit permit or replacement visa before you can leave. Ask your embassy: this step can take extra days in some countries.
- Tell your **airline** if you need to change your flight. Some fares allow changes for documented emergencies.
- If you were planning to continue to other countries, check whether they accept emergency documents. Many **do not** allow entry with an emergency travel document intended only for returning home.

## Step 6: once you are home

- Apply for a **full replacement passport** through the normal process.
- If your passport was stolen, watch out for **identity fraud**: check your bank accounts and credit reports.
- Claim on your **travel insurance** for extra costs (new flights, hotel nights, embassy fees, photos), with all receipts and the police report.
- Store your new passport details (number, issue date) somewhere safe.

## How to protect your passport while travelling

- Keep it in the **hotel safe** when you don't need it, and carry a **copy** instead when local law allows it.
- In crowded places, use an **inside pocket** or a money belt, not a back pocket or an open bag.
- Never hand it over as a **deposit** for a scooter or bike rental.
- Keep it in your **carry-on**, never in checked luggage: see [what to pack in a carry-on bag](/en/what-to-pack-in-a-carry-on-bag/).
- Check its **validity** before booking: many countries require at least **6 months** of validity after your arrival date.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until the day before the flight to contact the embassy.
- Forgetting about the visa or entry stamp that was in the lost passport.
- Using a passport that was found again after being reported lost: it has been cancelled.
- Not having any copy of the passport or any other ID.

## FAQ

### Can I fly home without a passport?

Not without a travel document. You need an **emergency travel document** or an emergency or temporary passport issued by your country.

### How long does it take to get an emergency passport?

Often **1 to 5 working days**, depending on the country, the embassy's workload and whether additional checks are needed. Some urgent cases are handled faster.

### Can I travel to another country with an emergency travel document?

Usually only to return home, sometimes with limited transits. Check the conditions printed on the document.

## Summary

If you lose your passport abroad: **search carefully**, **file a police report**, **contact your embassy or consulate**, and apply for an **emergency travel document** or **emergency passport**, bringing **photos, ID, a copy of the passport and proof of travel**. Check visa or entry stamp issues with local immigration, inform your airline and insurer, then apply for a full replacement once home. A photo of your passport stored securely before you travel makes everything faster.
