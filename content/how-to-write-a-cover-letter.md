A good cover letter is **short, tailored to the job and focused on what you can bring to the employer**. Aim for **one page**, with a clear structure: a strong opening, proof of your relevant skills, why this company, and a confident closing. Here is how to write one step by step.

## What is a cover letter for?

Your resume lists what you have done. Your cover letter explains **why you are a good fit for this specific job** and why you want it. It shows your motivation, your writing skills and your understanding of the company.

Some employers read it carefully, others skim it. That is why it needs to be **clear and easy to scan**.

## Before you write: do your research

1. **Read the job ad carefully** and underline the key skills and requirements.
2. **Learn about the company**: its products, values, recent news and the team you would join.
3. **Pick two or three achievements** from your experience that match the job's needs.

This preparation is what turns a generic letter into a convincing one.

## The four-part structure

### 1. The opening

Say which position you are applying for and grab attention with **your main selling point** or a genuine reason you are interested. Avoid starting with a bland "I am writing to apply for...".

*Example: "With three years of experience supporting customers of a fast-growing software company, I would love to bring my problem-solving skills to your support team."*

### 2. What you bring

This is the heart of the letter. Show, with **concrete examples**, how your skills match the job. Use results when you have them, such as a project you delivered, a process you improved or feedback you received. Focus on the employer's needs rather than your own wishes.

### 3. Why this company

Explain what attracts you to **this organization in particular**: its mission, its products, its way of working. One or two specific, sincere sentences are enough.

### 4. The closing

Thank the reader, express your wish to discuss your application in an interview and sign off politely.

## Format and length

- **One page maximum**, often three or four short paragraphs.
- A clear, professional font and plenty of white space.
- **Address a named person** when you can find the hiring manager's name.
- Save it as a PDF unless the employer asks otherwise, with a clear file name including your name.

Follow any specific instructions in the job ad: some employers ask for answers to particular questions in the letter.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sending the same letter to every employer**: recruiters notice immediately.
- **Repeating your resume** instead of explaining and adding context.
- **Talking only about yourself** and what the job would do for you.
- **Typos and wrong company names**: always proofread, ideally out loud, and ask someone to reread it.
- **Being too long or too vague**: every sentence should earn its place.

## Adapting to your situation

- **Recent graduate**: highlight internships, projects, volunteering and transferable skills.
- **Career changer**: explain your motivation clearly and connect past experience to the new field.
- **Freelancer returning to employment**: present your independent work as real experience. If you are going the other way, see [how to start freelancing](/en/how-to-start-freelancing/).

## After sending it

Prepare for the next step. If you get an interview, you will almost certainly be asked to introduce yourself: here is [how to answer "Tell me about yourself"](/en/how-to-answer-tell-me-about-yourself/).

## Summary

Write a one-page cover letter tailored to each job: open with your main selling point, prove your fit with concrete examples, explain why this company and close by asking for an interview. Research the employer first, avoid repeating your resume and proofread carefully before sending.
