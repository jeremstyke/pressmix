How to Get Rid of Hiccups: Simple Tricks That May Help

Hiccups that will not stop? Learn why they happen, simple tricks that may help you get rid of hiccups quickly, how to prevent them and when to see a doctor.

Published on November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

  2. Tricks to stop hiccups
  3. How to prevent hiccups
  4. Hiccups in babies
  5. When to see a doctor
  6. Summary

Most hiccups go away on their own within a few minutes. To help them stop, many people try holding their breath briefly, sipping cold water slowly or breathing into cupped hands. These tricks are not scientifically proven for everyone, but they are simple and harmless for most people. Hiccups that last more than 48 hours should be checked by a doctor.

What causes hiccups?

A hiccup is an involuntary contraction of the diaphragm, the muscle under your lungs that helps you breathe. Right after the contraction, the vocal cords close suddenly, which makes the typical "hic" sound.

Common triggers include:

  • eating too fast or eating a large meal;
  • fizzy drinks and swallowing air;
  • alcohol;
  • spicy food;
  • sudden temperature changes, such as a cold drink after a hot meal;
  • excitement, stress or laughing a lot.

Often, there is no obvious reason at all. Hiccups can be annoying or embarrassing, but a short bout is usually nothing to worry about.

Tricks to stop hiccups

These methods aim to reset your breathing rhythm or stimulate the nerves involved. Results vary from person to person, so try a few.

Breathing techniques

  • Hold your breath for a short time, then breathe out slowly. Repeat a few times.
  • Breathe slowly and deeply, counting to five on the in-breath and the out-breath.
  • Breathe into your cupped hands for a short time.
  • Bring your knees to your chest while sitting and lean forward for a moment, which gently compresses the diaphragm.

Drinking and eating

  • Sip a glass of cold water slowly, without stopping.
  • Gargle with cold water.
  • Swallow a teaspoon of sugar or suck on an ice cube.
  • Bite into a slice of lemon.

Other approaches

  • Distract yourself: hiccups sometimes stop when you stop paying attention to them.
  • Relax your shoulders and try to calm down if stress set them off.

Avoid potentially dangerous "tricks", such as scaring someone or pressing hard on the eyes.

How to prevent hiccups

  • Eat slowly and chew well.
  • Choose smaller portions.
  • Limit fizzy drinks and alcohol.
  • Avoid very hot or very cold food and drinks one after the other.
  • Do not chew gum all day, which can make you swallow air.

Hiccups in babies

Hiccups are very common in babies, especially after feeding, and usually do not bother them. Burping during and after feeds may help. If your baby seems distressed, feeds poorly or vomits often, talk to a pediatrician.

When to see a doctor

Hiccups are almost always harmless. However, consult a doctor if they:

  • last more than 48 hours (persistent hiccups);
  • keep coming back often;
  • prevent you from eating, drinking or sleeping;
  • come with chest pain, difficulty breathing, abdominal pain, vomiting or other unusual symptoms.

Persistent hiccups can sometimes be linked to an underlying condition or to certain medicines, which a doctor can assess.

Healthy habits help in general: drink enough water during the day and protect your sleep with our tips on how to fall asleep faster.

Summary

Hiccups are involuntary contractions of the diaphragm, often triggered by eating too fast, fizzy drinks, alcohol or stress. They usually stop on their own, and simple tricks like holding your breath, sipping cold water or breathing slowly may help. Eat slowly to prevent them and see a doctor if they last more than 48 hours or come with other symptoms.
