What to Pack in a Carry-On Bag: The Complete Checklist

Traveling with hand luggage only? Here is what to pack in a carry-on bag, the liquid rules to know, what is banned and smart packing tips to fit everything in.

Published on November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

In a carry-on bag, pack your travel documents, medication, valuables, electronics and a change of clothes, plus toiletries in small containers that follow airport liquid rules. Everything you cannot afford to lose belongs in the cabin with you. Here is a full checklist and the rules to know before you go.

Check your airline's rules first

Carry-on allowances differ from one airline to another, and sometimes between fare types on the same airline. Before packing, check on the airline's website:

  • the maximum size of your bag;
  • the weight limit, if there is one;
  • whether you can bring a personal item (handbag, laptop bag, small backpack) in addition to your carry-on.

Budget airlines are often stricter, and an oversized bag at the gate can cost you an extra fee.

The essentials checklist

Documents and money

  • Passport or ID card, and visa if needed
  • Boarding pass (printed or on your phone)
  • Bank cards and a little cash
  • Travel insurance details and important phone numbers
  • Copies of documents, stored securely online

Health

  • Your prescription medication, in its original packaging, with a copy of the prescription if possible
  • Basic first aid: plasters, pain relief you normally use
  • Glasses or contact lenses

Electronics

  • Phone and charger
  • Headphones
  • Power bank: these generally must travel in the cabin, not in checked luggage
  • Travel adapter
  • Laptop or tablet if you need them

Comfort on board

  • A light sweater or scarf, as planes can be cold
  • An empty reusable water bottle to fill after security
  • A snack
  • A book, eye mask or neck pillow for long flights

The liquid rules

Many airports apply restrictions on liquids in hand luggage. The most common rule is:

  1. Each liquid must be in a container of 100 ml or less.
  2. All containers must fit in one clear, resealable plastic bag, usually about one litre.
  3. You may need to take this bag out at security.

"Liquids" include gels, creams, pastes and aerosols: toothpaste, sunscreen, mascara and even some foods. Some airports with newer scanners have relaxed these rules, but this is not the case everywhere, so check the rules of each airport on your route, including the return trip.

Medication and baby food needed during the trip are usually allowed in larger amounts, but you may have to show them.

What you cannot pack

  • Knives and sharp objects (rules vary for small scissors)
  • Tools and sports equipment that could be used as weapons
  • Flammable products and most gas canisters
  • Liquids over the allowed size

When in doubt, look at the "prohibited items" page of your airport or airline.

Packing tips to fit everything in

  • Roll your clothes instead of folding them to save space and limit creases.
  • Use packing cubes to stay organized.
  • Wear your heaviest shoes and jacket on the plane.
  • Choose clothes that match each other so you can mix outfits.
  • Buy toiletries at your destination if the trip is long.

Traveling with hand luggage only also helps you control costs, which fits well with a monthly budget. And if you are flying with children, our guide on screen time for kids can help you plan a calm journey.

Summary

Pack documents, medication, valuables, electronics and a change of clothes in your carry-on, and keep liquids in containers of 100 ml or less in a clear bag unless your airports say otherwise. Check your airline's size and weight limits before leaving, roll your clothes and wear your bulkiest items to make everything fit.
