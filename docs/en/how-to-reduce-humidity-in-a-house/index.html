How to Reduce Humidity in a House: Causes and Solutions

Condensation, musty smells, mold? Here is how to reduce humidity in a house with airing, ventilation, a dehumidifier and daily habits, and when to call a pro.

Published on November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

To reduce humidity in a house, air out every room daily, use extractor fans in the kitchen and bathroom, dry laundry outside or in a ventilated room and keep a steady temperature. If moisture persists, a dehumidifier helps, and any leak or rising damp must be fixed at the source. Here is how to identify the cause and choose the right solution.

Signs your home is too humid

  • Condensation on windows, especially in the morning.
  • A musty smell in some rooms or cupboards.
  • Black spots of mold on walls, ceilings, window frames or silicone seals.
  • Peeling paint or wallpaper.
  • Clothes that feel damp or smell stale in the wardrobe.

A simple hygrometer, which is inexpensive, shows the relative humidity of a room. Many sources recommend keeping indoor humidity roughly between 40% and 60%.

Where does the moisture come from?

Everyday activities

Breathing, cooking, showering and drying clothes indoors all release water vapor. In a poorly ventilated home, this moisture has nowhere to go and condenses on cold surfaces.

Structural problems

  • Leaks from roofs, pipes or gutters.
  • Rising damp, when ground moisture climbs up walls.
  • Penetrating damp through cracks in walls or poor seals around windows.

Structural problems need repairs: no amount of airing will fix a leaking pipe.

Solutions you can apply today

1. Air out every day

Open windows wide for 5 to 10 minutes once or twice a day, even in winter. A short, strong airing renews the air without cooling the walls too much.

2. Use extractor fans

Turn on the kitchen hood while cooking and the bathroom fan during and after showers. Keep vents clean and never block them.

3. Cover pans when cooking

Lids reduce steam and save energy at the same time.

4. Dry laundry carefully

Dry clothes outside when possible. Indoors, choose a well-ventilated room, open the window slightly, or use a dryer that is properly vented.

5. Keep a steady temperature

Cold rooms attract condensation. Try to heat the home evenly rather than leaving some rooms very cold.

6. Leave space around furniture

Move wardrobes and sofas a few centimetres away from outside walls to let air circulate.

Equipment that helps

7. Use a dehumidifier

An electric dehumidifier removes moisture from the air and is effective in problem rooms. Choose a size suited to the room and empty the tank regularly.

8. Moisture absorbers

Small absorbers using calcium chloride crystals can help in cupboards and small spaces, but they are not enough for a whole room.

9. Improve ventilation

If your home has a mechanical ventilation system, check that it works and that filters and vents are clean.

Dealing with mold

For small areas, clean mold with a suitable product, wearing gloves and keeping the room ventilated. Large or recurring mold is a sign of a deeper problem. Damp and mold can affect health, particularly for people with asthma or allergies, so seek advice if anyone at home has symptoms.

When to call a professional

Contact a professional if you suspect a leak, rising damp or a ventilation fault, or if mold keeps coming back despite good habits. If you rent, inform your landlord in writing. Repairs can be costly, which is one more reason to keep an emergency fund.

Summary

Reduce humidity by airing out daily, using extractor fans, drying laundry carefully and keeping a steady temperature. A dehumidifier helps in problem rooms. Fix leaks and structural damp at the source, and call a professional if mold keeps returning.
