How to Make a Monthly Budget (Simple Step-by-Step Method)

Making a monthly budget takes less than an hour. Here is a simple 5-step method, the 50/30/20 rule and the mistakes to avoid so you finally take control of your money.

A monthly budget is not about depriving yourself. It is about knowing where your money goes so you can decide what to do with it. The good news: the method fits in five steps and takes about an hour to set up.

Why make a monthly budget?

Without a budget, most people underestimate their spending by 20 to 30%. Small purchases, forgotten subscriptions and irregular costs (gifts, repairs) quietly drain the account by the end of the month.

A budget helps you:

  • know exactly how much you can spend each month;
  • save money before you spend it;
  • spot useless expenses;
  • prepare for big expenses without stress.

Step 1: Add up your net income

Add everything that lands in your account each month: net salary, benefits, pensions, side income. If your income varies, use the average of the last three months, or better, your lowest month to stay on the safe side.

Step 2: List your fixed expenses

These come back every month for roughly the same amount:

  • rent or mortgage;
  • electricity, gas, water;
  • insurance (home, car, health);
  • phone and internet;
  • loans;
  • subscriptions (streaming, gym, news).

Open your last three bank statements and write down every direct debit. This is often where you find a forgotten subscription.

Step 3: Estimate your variable expenses

Groceries, fuel, eating out, clothes and hobbies change from month to month. Take a three-month average. Do not forget yearly expenses (car service, holidays, insurance renewals): divide them by 12 and add them to your monthly budget.

Step 4: Apply the 50/30/20 rule

The 50/30/20 rule is the simplest way to split your income:

  1. 50% for needs: housing, bills, groceries, transport.
  2. 30% for wants: going out, hobbies, shopping, travel.
  3. 20% for savings: emergency fund, projects, paying off debt early.

Example with $2,000 net per month: $1,000 for needs, $600 for wants and $400 for savings.

If your needs go over 50%, that is fine: adjust the split (for example 60/25/15) and work on reducing your biggest costs over time.

Step 5: Pay yourself first

People who really save have one secret: they put money aside the day their salary arrives, not at the end of the month. Set up an automatic transfer to a savings account the day after payday. What is left in your current account is what you can spend.

Which tool should you use?

Three options depending on your style:

  • A spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets): free and fully customizable.
  • Your bank's app: most of them already sort your spending automatically.
  • The envelope method: one envelope (or sub-account) per spending category. When the envelope is empty, you stop.

The best tool is the one you will actually use. Ten minutes a week is enough to stay on track.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Being too strict: a budget with no fun gets abandoned after two months. Keep some room for wants.
  • Forgetting irregular expenses: they are what blows up most budgets.
  • Having no emergency fund: aim for three months of expenses to handle the unexpected.
  • Never reviewing your budget: your income and expenses change, so should your budget.

Summary

To make a monthly budget, add up your income, list your fixed and variable expenses, split your money (for example with the 50/30/20 rule) and save at the start of the month. Start simple, adjust every month, and within a few weeks you will know exactly where your money goes.
