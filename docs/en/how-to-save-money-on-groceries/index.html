How to Save Money on Groceries: 12 Tips That Really Work

Food bills keep rising? Here are 12 practical tips to save money on groceries, from meal planning and lists to store brands, bulk buying and cutting food waste.

Published on November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

Contents

  2. In the store
  3. At home
  4. Use loyalty programs wisely
  5. Summary

To save money on groceries, plan your meals for the week, shop with a list, compare unit prices and choose store brands. Cooking more at home and wasting less food also make a big difference. None of these tips require extreme couponing: they are simple habits that add up over a month.

Before you go shopping

1. Set a grocery budget

Decide how much you want to spend each week or month. A clear limit makes every choice easier. If you do not have one yet, start with our guide to making a monthly budget.

2. Plan your meals

Plan the main meals of the week before you shop. Meal planning helps you buy only what you need and use the same ingredients in several dishes. Cook a big pot of soup, chili or pasta sauce and eat it twice.

3. Check what you already have

Look in the fridge, freezer and cupboards first. Build meals around what needs using up.

4. Write a shopping list and stick to it

Impulse purchases are one of the biggest budget leaks. A list keeps you focused. Some people find that shopping online with a fixed basket helps them avoid temptation.

5. Do not shop hungry

It is a classic for a reason: when you are hungry, snacks and treats are much more tempting.

In the store

6. Compare unit prices

The shelf label often shows the price per kilo, litre or unit. This is the only fair way to compare different pack sizes and brands. The bigger pack is not always cheaper.

7. Try store brands

Store brands (own labels) are often noticeably cheaper than big brands, and for basics like flour, rice, pasta, canned vegetables or cleaning products, the difference in quality is often small. Test them one product at a time.

8. Look high and low

The most expensive products are often placed at eye level. Cheaper options are frequently on the top or bottom shelves.

9. Buy seasonal and frozen produce

Seasonal fruit and vegetables are usually cheaper and tastier. Frozen vegetables and fruit are a good alternative: they keep for months and reduce waste.

10. Buy in bulk, but only what you use

Buying large quantities saves money only on products you use regularly and that keep well, such as rice, pasta, oats or toilet paper. Fresh food bought in bulk often ends up in the bin.

At home

11. Reduce food waste

Wasted food is wasted money. Store food correctly, eat the oldest items first and freeze leftovers. Our guides on how to keep bread fresh and how long eggs last can help.

12. Cook more from scratch

Ready meals and pre-cut ingredients cost more. Simple homemade dishes like soups, omelets, stews and pancakes are often cheaper and healthier. Batch cooking on the weekend saves time during the week.

Use loyalty programs wisely

Loyalty cards and apps can offer real discounts, but only if you were going to buy the product anyway. A promotion on something you do not need is not a saving. Also check that "2 for 1" deals actually lower the unit price.

Summary

Save money on groceries by setting a budget, planning meals, shopping with a list and never on an empty stomach. In the store, compare unit prices, try store brands and buy seasonal or frozen produce. At home, cut food waste and cook more from scratch. Together, these habits can noticeably lower your food bill.
