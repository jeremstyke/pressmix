How to Check Tire Pressure (and What the Right Pressure Is)

Learn how to check tire pressure in five minutes, where to find the right pressure for your car and why it matters for safety, fuel use and tire life.

To check tire pressure, use a tire gauge on cold tires, compare the reading with the pressure recommended by your car's manufacturer, then add or release air as needed. The right pressure is not the number printed on the tire itself: it is shown on a sticker on the car, often inside the driver's door frame. Here is how to do it step by step.

Why tire pressure matters

Correct tire pressure is one of the simplest ways to stay safe on the road. Tires that are underinflated or overinflated can cause:

  • longer braking distances and less grip, especially on wet roads;
  • uneven and faster tire wear;
  • higher fuel consumption when tires are underinflated;
  • a greater risk of a blowout in extreme cases.

Where to find the right pressure

The recommended pressure is set by your car's manufacturer. You can usually find it:

  • on a sticker inside the driver's door frame;
  • inside the fuel filler flap;
  • in the owner's manual.

It is often given in PSI or bar and may differ between the front and rear tires. Some cars also list a higher pressure for a fully loaded vehicle.

The number molded on the side of the tire is the maximum pressure the tire can handle, not the one you should use.

When to check

  • About once a month, as tires slowly lose air naturally.
  • Before a long trip or when carrying a heavy load.
  • When the temperature drops sharply, since cold air makes pressure fall.
  • If your dashboard's tire pressure warning light comes on.

Always check when the tires are cold: before driving or after the car has been parked for a few hours. After driving, the air inside heats up and the reading will be higher.

How to check tire pressure step by step

  1. Get a tire gauge. Digital and dial gauges are inexpensive and more reliable than many old gas station gauges.
  2. Remove the valve cap from the first tire and keep it somewhere safe.
  3. Press the gauge firmly onto the valve until the hissing stops.
  4. Read the pressure and compare it with the recommended value.
  5. Repeat for every tire, and do not forget the spare if you have one.
  6. Put the valve caps back on to keep dirt and moisture out.

How to add or release air

  • To add air: use an air pump at a gas station, a compressor or a portable inflator. Add air in short bursts and check the pressure again until you reach the right value.
  • To release air: press the small pin in the center of the valve briefly with the tip of the gauge or a fingernail, then measure again.

Many station pumps let you set the target pressure and stop automatically, but double-check with your own gauge.

Signs of a problem

If one tire keeps losing pressure much faster than the others, it may have a puncture, a damaged valve or a problem with the rim. Have it checked by a professional rather than simply refilling it again and again.

Also inspect your tires for cracks, bulges, nails or worn tread when you check the pressure.

A habit that pays off

Well-inflated tires last longer and help limit fuel costs, which makes a difference over a year in a monthly budget. Unexpected repairs still happen, so an emergency fund is useful for every driver.

Summary

Check your tire pressure about once a month on cold tires, using a gauge and the pressure listed on your car's door sticker or manual. Add or release air until each tire matches the recommendation, replace the valve caps and have any tire that keeps losing air inspected by a professional.
