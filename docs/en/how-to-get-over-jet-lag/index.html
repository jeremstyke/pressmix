How to Get Over Jet Lag Faster: Before, During and After Your Flight

Crossing time zones? Here is how to get over jet lag faster with practical tips before you fly, on the plane and after landing: light, sleep, naps and water.

To get over jet lag faster, switch to your destination's schedule as soon as possible: eat and sleep at local times, get daylight at the right moments, keep naps short and stay hydrated. Preparing a few days before departure also helps. Your body clock adjusts gradually, so expect a few days before you feel fully normal.

What is jet lag?

Jet lag happens when you cross several time zones quickly. Your internal body clock stays on your home time while the sun and the local schedule follow a new rhythm. The result can be:

  • trouble sleeping at night and sleepiness during the day;
  • tiredness and difficulty concentrating;
  • reduced appetite or digestive problems;
  • irritability.

It is generally said that the body adjusts by roughly a day for each time zone crossed, though this varies from person to person. Many people find traveling east harder than traveling west, because it is harder to fall asleep earlier than usual.

Before you fly

Shift your schedule gradually

A few days before departure, move your bedtime and wake-up time a little towards your destination's time:

  • Traveling east: go to bed and get up a bit earlier.
  • Traveling west: go to bed and get up a bit later.

Arrive rested

Avoid starting the trip already exhausted. Pack early (our carry-on checklist helps) and try to sleep well the nights before.

Choose your flight time if you can

For eastbound trips, an evening flight that lets you sleep on board and arrive in the morning can work well. For westbound trips, arriving in the evening often makes it easier to go straight to bed.

On the plane

  • Set your watch to destination time as soon as you board.
  • Sleep or stay awake according to the time at your destination.
  • Use an eye mask, earplugs or noise-canceling headphones to rest.
  • Drink water regularly. Cabin air is dry, and dehydration makes fatigue worse. See how much water you should drink a day.
  • Limit alcohol and caffeine, which disturb sleep.
  • Walk around the cabin and stretch when it is safe to do so.

After landing

Use light wisely

Light is the strongest signal for your body clock. As a general rule:

  • After flying east, get daylight in the morning and avoid bright light in the evening.
  • After flying west, get daylight in the late afternoon and evening.

A walk outside is one of the best things you can do.

Live on local time

Eat meals at local times, even if you are not very hungry, and avoid going to bed too early in the afternoon.

Keep naps short

If you really need a nap, keep it to about 20 to 30 minutes and not too late in the day, otherwise you will struggle to sleep at night.

Sleep well at night

Make your room dark, cool and quiet, and put screens away before bed. Our tips on how to fall asleep faster work just as well in a hotel room.

What about melatonin and sleeping pills?

Melatonin is sometimes used for jet lag, and rules for buying it vary by country. Sleeping pills can have side effects. Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking either, especially if you take other medication.

Short trips

If you only stay a day or two, it may be better to stay on your home schedule as much as possible and plan important meetings for times when you will be alert.

Summary

Prepare by shifting your schedule a few days before you leave, switch to destination time on the plane, drink water and limit alcohol and caffeine. After landing, use daylight at the right times, eat and sleep on local time and keep naps short. Ask a health professional before using melatonin or sleeping pills.
