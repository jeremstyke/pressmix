How to Keep Bread Fresh for Longer (and Revive Stale Bread)

Why bread goes stale, where to store it, why the fridge is a bad idea and how to freeze and revive it: easy tips to keep bread fresh for longer and waste less.

Published on November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

Contents

  2. The best ways to store bread at room temperature
  3. Why you should avoid the fridge
  4. How to freeze bread properly
  5. How to revive stale bread
  6. Ideas for leftover bread
  7. Summary

To keep bread fresh, store it at room temperature in a bread box, a cloth bag or a paper bag, cut side down, and freeze whatever you will not eat within a couple of days. Avoid the fridge: it makes bread go stale faster. Here is how to choose the right method for each type of bread.

Why does bread go stale?

Stale bread is not only about drying out. As bread cools and ages, the starch inside it slowly recrystallizes, a process called retrogradation. The crumb becomes firm and crumbly, and the crust loses its crunch.

Two other problems can appear:

  • Drying out if the bread is left uncovered;
  • Mold if it is kept in a warm, humid, airtight place.

The goal is to find a balance between protecting the bread from air and letting it breathe a little.

The best ways to store bread at room temperature

Bread box

A bread box keeps some humidity around the loaf while allowing a little airflow. It is one of the best options for crusty and homemade bread. Clean it regularly and remove crumbs to limit mold.

Cloth or paper bag

A linen or cotton bag works well for baguettes and crusty loaves eaten within a day or two. A paper bag keeps the crust crisp but lets the bread dry out faster.

Cut side down

Once you slice a loaf, place it cut side down on a wooden board. The crust protects the rest of the bread and the cut surface dries out more slowly.

What about plastic bags?

Plastic keeps bread soft but traps moisture, which softens the crust and can encourage mold. It suits sandwich bread and soft rolls, which are usually sold in plastic anyway.

Why you should avoid the fridge

It sounds logical, but the fridge is one of the worst places for bread. At fridge temperatures, staling happens faster than at room temperature. Only consider it in very hot, humid weather to slow mold, and toast the bread before eating it.

How to freeze bread properly

Freezing is the best way to keep bread for a long time.

  1. Freeze it fresh, on the day you buy or bake it.
  2. Slice it first so you can take out only what you need.
  3. Wrap it tightly in a freezer bag, removing as much air as possible.
  4. Label it with the date.

To use it, toast slices straight from the freezer, or let a loaf thaw at room temperature in its bag, then warm it briefly in the oven to bring back the crust.

How to revive stale bread

Bread that is a day or two old can often be saved:

  • Sprinkle or quickly run the loaf under water, then put it in a hot oven for a few minutes. The steam softens the crumb and the heat crisps the crust. Eat it soon after, as it will stale again quickly.
  • Toast it: toasting hides staleness very well.

Ideas for leftover bread

Never throw away bread that is only dry (moldy bread, however, should go in the bin):

  • breadcrumbs, made by blitzing dried bread;
  • croutons for soups and salads;
  • French toast;
  • bread pudding;
  • toasted bread for bruschetta.

Using leftovers is an easy way to save money on groceries and fits well into a monthly budget. For more storage tips, see how to store fresh herbs.

Summary

Keep bread at room temperature in a bread box or cloth bag, cut side down, and avoid the fridge. Freeze sliced bread on the day you buy it if you will not finish it quickly. Revive slightly stale bread in a hot oven and turn dry leftovers into croutons, breadcrumbs or French toast.
