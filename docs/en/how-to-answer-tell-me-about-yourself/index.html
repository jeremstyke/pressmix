How to Answer "Tell Me About Yourself" in a Job Interview

Tell me about yourself" opens most job interviews. Here is a simple present, past, future structure, tips for every profile and the mistakes to avoid.

To answer "Tell me about yourself", give a short professional summary of one to two minutes using a simple structure: who you are now, what you have done before, and why you want this job. The goal is not to tell your life story but to show why you are a good fit for the role. Here is how to prepare an answer that sounds natural and convincing.

Why recruiters ask this question

This question is rarely just an icebreaker. It lets the interviewer:

  • see how you communicate and organize your ideas;
  • understand which parts of your background you consider important;
  • check whether your profile matches the job;
  • find topics to explore in the rest of the interview.

Your answer sets the tone for everything that follows, so it is worth preparing.

The present, past, future structure

This simple framework works for almost every profile.

1. Present: who you are today

Start with your current role or situation and one or two key strengths.

"I'm a customer support specialist with a focus on software products, and I enjoy solving complex problems for clients."

2. Past: what brought you here

Pick two or three experiences or achievements that are relevant to the job. Focus on results and skills rather than listing every position.

"Before that, I worked in retail, where I learned to stay calm with demanding customers. In my current job, I helped create a guide that new team members now use."

3. Future: why this job

Finish by linking your background to the position you are applying for.

"I'm now looking for a role where I can take on more responsibility, and your team's work on customer experience really appeals to me."

Adapt your answer to your situation

  • Recent graduate: talk about your studies, internships, projects, volunteering or part-time jobs, and the skills they gave you.
  • Career changer: explain what motivates the change and highlight transferable skills, such as communication, organization or project management.
  • Returning after a break: mention the break briefly and positively, then focus on what you bring now.
  • Experienced professional: select only the most relevant highlights rather than covering your whole career.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reciting your resume line by line: the recruiter already has it.
  • Talking too long: beyond two minutes, attention drops.
  • Going too personal: family details, hobbies or your childhood are rarely relevant, unless they connect clearly to the job.
  • Being negative about a former employer.
  • Learning a script by heart: it sounds robotic. Memorize the structure and key points instead.

How to prepare

  1. Read the job ad carefully and note the main skills required.
  2. Choose two or three experiences that prove those skills.
  3. Write a short version of your answer, then say it out loud.
  4. Time yourself and cut what is not essential.
  5. Practice with a friend or record yourself to check your tone and pace.

Also prepare a shorter version of about 30 seconds for networking events or phone screenings.

Body language matters too

Look at the interviewer, sit comfortably and speak at a calm pace. Smiling when you talk about what you enjoy makes your answer more engaging. A good night's sleep before the big day helps too: see our tips on how to fall asleep faster.

If you are thinking about working for yourself instead, read our guide on how to start freelancing. And while you are job hunting, a solid emergency fund can reduce the pressure.

Summary

Answer "Tell me about yourself" in one to two minutes with a present, past, future structure: your current role, two or three relevant achievements and why you want this job. Tailor it to the role, avoid reciting your resume and practice out loud until it sounds natural.
