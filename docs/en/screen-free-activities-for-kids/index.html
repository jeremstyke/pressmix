Screen-Free Activities for Kids: 20 Easy Ideas at Home and Outside

Looking for screen-free activities for kids? Here are 20 simple ideas for home and outdoors, sorted by situation, plus tips to make the switch from screens.

Published on November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

Contents

  2. Calm activities
  3. Active games indoors
  4. Activities outside
  5. Involve them in everyday life
  6. Board and card games for every age
  7. Tips to make screen-free time easier
  8. Summary

The best screen-free activities for kids are often the simplest: building, drawing, reading, cooking together, playing outside and board games. Keep a few ready-to-use ideas and materials at hand, and children will turn to them more easily than to a tablet. Here are 20 ideas sorted by situation.

Creative activities at home

  • Drawing and painting: keep paper, crayons and washable paints in an easy-to-reach box.
  • Building with blocks or bricks: give a challenge, such as the tallest tower or a house for a toy.
  • Cardboard creations: an empty box can become a car, a castle or a puppet theater.
  • Modeling dough: you can even make it at home with flour, salt and water.
  • Collages: old magazines, scissors suited to their age and glue are enough.

Calm activities

  • Reading together, or letting older kids read alone in a cozy reading corner.
  • Puzzles, adapted to the child's age.
  • Audio stories and podcasts for kids: no screen, lots of imagination.
  • Coloring or sticker books, great for quiet moments and journeys.
  • Listening to music and guessing the instruments.

Active games indoors

  • Indoor obstacle course with cushions, chairs and blankets.
  • Dancing to their favorite songs, or a freeze dance game.
  • Hide and seek or a treasure hunt with simple clues.
  • Building a fort with sheets and chairs.

Activities outside

  • A nature walk with a small mission: collect leaves, spot birds or find five different stones.
  • Bike, scooter or ball games in a park.
  • Gardening: planting seeds in a pot is enough to start.
  • A family run or race game: if you want to make it a habit, see how to start running as a beginner.

Involve them in everyday life

  • Cooking together: washing vegetables, mixing batter, decorating pancakes. Our recipe for easy homemade crepes is perfect for little helpers.
  • Small household jobs: sorting socks, watering plants, setting the table. Children often love feeling useful.

Board and card games for every age

Board games teach patience, turn-taking and losing gracefully. Memory games and simple card games suit young children, while older kids can enjoy strategy and cooperative games. A weekly family game night is an easy ritual to set up.

Tips to make screen-free time easier

  • Let them be bored: boredom often sparks creativity. You do not need to fill every minute.
  • Prepare an activity box with materials and idea cards they can pick from.
  • Rotate toys: put some away for a few weeks and bring them back later, they will feel new.
  • Set clear screen rules for the whole family, as explained in our guide on screen time for kids.
  • Join in at first: children often need an adult to launch an activity before they continue alone.
  • Lead by example by putting your own phone away.

Adapt to the child's age

Toddlers need simple, sensory activities and close supervision, while school-age children enjoy challenges, projects and some independence. Always check that materials are safe for the child's age, especially small parts for children under three.

Summary

Screen-free activities do not need to be complicated: drawing, building, reading, active games, outdoor walks, cooking and board games keep children busy and engaged. Prepare materials in advance, accept a little boredom, set clear family screen rules and join in to get things started.
