How to Store Fresh Herbs So They Last Longer

Basil, parsley, coriander, rosemary: here is how to store fresh herbs so they last longer, in a glass of water or a damp paper towel, plus how to freeze them.

To store fresh herbs, treat soft herbs like a bouquet of flowers, with their stems in a glass of water, and wrap woody herbs in a slightly damp paper towel in the fridge. Basil is the exception: it prefers room temperature. With the right method, herbs stay fresh for days longer instead of wilting.

Two families of herbs

The best method depends on the type of herb.

  • Soft (tender) herbs: parsley, coriander (cilantro), mint, dill, tarragon, chives and basil. Their stems are soft and they wilt quickly.
  • Woody (hardy) herbs: rosemary, thyme, sage and oregano. Their stems are firm and they dry out rather than wilt.

First steps for all herbs

As soon as you get home:

  1. Remove the packaging, elastic bands and any yellowed or slimy leaves.
  2. Do not wash them yet unless they are very dirty. Moisture speeds up spoilage. Wash them just before using them.
  3. If you do wash them, dry them thoroughly with a salad spinner or a clean towel.

Soft herbs: the glass of water method

This works well for parsley, coriander, mint and dill.

  1. Trim the ends of the stems.
  2. Put the herbs in a glass or jar with a little water, like flowers.
  3. Loosely cover the leaves with a plastic bag or a reusable food bag.
  4. Store the jar in the fridge.
  5. Change the water every couple of days.

The basil exception

Basil does not like the cold: its leaves turn black in the fridge. Keep it in a glass of water on the kitchen counter, away from direct sunlight. A potted basil plant on a sunny windowsill is often the easiest solution.

Woody herbs: the paper towel method

Rosemary, thyme and sage keep better slightly dry.

  1. Wrap them loosely in a slightly damp paper towel.
  2. Place the bundle in a container or a food bag, not completely sealed.
  3. Store in the vegetable drawer of the fridge.

Check them every few days and replace the paper towel if it is too wet.

How to freeze fresh herbs

Freezing is perfect when you have too many herbs. The texture changes, so frozen herbs are best for cooked dishes such as soups, sauces and stews.

  • Chopped, in ice cube trays: chop the herbs, fill an ice cube tray, top with water or olive oil, then freeze. Drop a cube straight into the pan.
  • Whole, in a bag: lay sprigs flat in a freezer bag, remove the air and freeze. Woody herbs freeze well this way.

Label the bags with the name and date.

How to dry herbs

Drying suits woody herbs best, as they keep much of their flavor.

  1. Tie a small bunch with string.
  2. Hang it upside down in a dry, airy place away from sunlight.
  3. Once the leaves crumble easily, strip them and store them in an airtight jar.

Soft herbs like basil and parsley lose much of their aroma when dried; freezing is usually a better choice for them.

Signs your herbs have gone bad

Throw herbs away if they are slimy, moldy, black or smell unpleasant. Slightly wilted soft herbs can often be revived in cold water and used in cooked dishes.

Good storage helps reduce food waste and saves money, two goals that fit well with a monthly budget. For another everyday kitchen question, see how long eggs last.

Summary

Store soft herbs like flowers in a glass of water in the fridge, keep basil on the counter and wrap woody herbs in a damp paper towel in the vegetable drawer. Wash herbs only before use, freeze extras in ice cube trays and dry woody herbs to enjoy them for months.
