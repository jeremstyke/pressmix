How to Start Freelancing With No Experience: A Step-by-Step Guide

Want to work for yourself? Here is how to start freelancing step by step: choosing a service, finding first clients, setting your rates and handling the admin.

To start freelancing, choose one clear service you can deliver well, build a small portfolio, set a simple rate, then find your first clients through your network and freelance platforms. Before you quit a job, check the legal and tax rules in your country and build a financial cushion. Here is a practical roadmap.

Step 1: Choose what you will offer

Freelancers who succeed early usually start with one specific service rather than "I can do anything". Look at:

  • the skills you already have from work, studies or hobbies;
  • what people already ask you for help with;
  • services businesses pay for regularly: writing, design, web development, translation, bookkeeping, marketing, video editing, virtual assistance.

A clear offer such as "I write blog articles for small hotels" is easier to sell than a vague one.

Step 2: Build a simple portfolio

Clients want proof that you can do the job. If you are starting from zero:

  • create two or three sample projects that show your skills;
  • offer to help a local association or a friend's business and ask for a testimonial;
  • present your work on a simple website, a portfolio platform or a professional social profile.

Quality matters more than quantity. A few strong examples beat a long list of average ones.

Step 3: Set your rates

Pricing is one of the hardest parts. To find a starting point:

  1. Research what other freelancers charge for similar work in your market.
  2. Calculate your costs: equipment, software, insurance, taxes and social contributions.
  3. Remember that you will not bill every hour you work: admin, prospecting and training take time too.

You can charge by the hour, by the day or by the project. Project pricing often works well once you know how long tasks take. Avoid setting prices so low that you burn out.

Step 4: Handle the legal and admin side

Rules for self-employed workers vary a lot between countries. Before you start, find out:

  • whether you need to register a business or a self-employed status;
  • how income tax and social contributions work for freelancers;
  • whether you need to charge sales tax or VAT;
  • whether you need professional insurance.

Use a separate bank account for your freelance income and keep every invoice and receipt. An accountant or your local business support office can help you choose the right setup.

Step 5: Find your first clients

  • Tell your network: former colleagues, friends and family often bring the first projects.
  • Use freelance platforms to gain experience and reviews, even if fees and competition are high.
  • Contact businesses directly with a short, personalized message explaining how you can help them.
  • Share your work on professional networks to show your expertise.

When you talk to a client, use a clear introduction of who you are and what you do. Our tips on answering "tell me about yourself" work for client calls too.

Step 6: Protect yourself with contracts

Even for small jobs, put the essentials in writing: scope of work, deadlines, price, number of revisions and payment terms. Asking for a deposit before starting is common and protects you from unpaid work.

Step 7: Manage irregular income

Freelance income often goes up and down. To stay calm:

  • set aside money for taxes each time you are paid;
  • build an emergency fund covering several months of expenses;
  • pay yourself a regular "salary" from your business account and follow a monthly budget.

Many people start freelancing on the side while keeping their job, until their income becomes stable enough.

Summary

Start freelancing by choosing one clear service, building a small portfolio and setting rates based on market research and your real costs. Check the legal and tax rules in your country, find first clients through your network and platforms, use simple contracts and save for taxes and lean months.
