What Is a Crypto Wallet and How Does It Work?

What is a crypto wallet? Learn how it works, hot vs cold wallets, custodial vs self-custody options and the key rules to keep your crypto safe from scams.

A crypto wallet is a tool that stores the private keys giving you access to your cryptocurrencies. Your coins do not actually sit inside the wallet: they are recorded on the blockchain, and the wallet holds the keys that prove they are yours and let you send them. Whoever controls those keys controls the funds.

How a crypto wallet works

Every wallet relies on a pair of keys:

  • The public key, from which your address is created. You can share this address to receive crypto, a bit like an account number.
  • The private key, which signs transactions and proves you own the funds. It must never be shared with anyone.

When you send crypto, the wallet uses your private key to sign the transaction, which is then broadcast to the network and recorded on the blockchain.

The recovery phrase (seed phrase)

When you create a self-custody wallet, it usually gives you a recovery phrase of 12 or 24 words. This phrase can rebuild all your keys if you lose your phone or device.

It is the most important thing to protect:

  • Write it down on paper and keep it somewhere safe, offline.
  • Never type it into a website, an email or a chat.
  • Never take a photo of it or store it in the cloud.
  • No legitimate support team will ever ask for it.

Anyone who gets your recovery phrase can empty your wallet, and transactions on the blockchain generally cannot be reversed.

Custodial vs self-custody wallets

Custodial wallets

When you buy crypto on an exchange and leave it there, the platform holds the keys for you. It is convenient and you can usually recover access with a password reset. The downside: you depend on the platform. If it is hacked, goes bankrupt or freezes withdrawals, your funds may be at risk.

Self-custody wallets

With a self-custody (or non-custodial) wallet, you alone hold the keys. You have full control, but also full responsibility: if you lose your recovery phrase, nobody can help you get your funds back.

Hot wallets vs cold wallets

Hot wallets

Hot wallets are connected to the internet: mobile apps, browser extensions and desktop software. They are practical for frequent transactions and small amounts, but more exposed to malware and phishing.

Cold wallets

Cold wallets keep your keys offline. The most common are hardware wallets, small devices that sign transactions without exposing the private key to your computer. They are generally considered safer for larger amounts held over the long term.

How to choose a wallet

Ask yourself a few questions:

  1. How much are you holding? Small amounts can sit in a reputable hot wallet; larger ones are often better protected in a hardware wallet.
  2. Which cryptocurrencies? Not every wallet supports every blockchain.
  3. How often do you transact? Frequent use favors a hot wallet.
  4. Are you comfortable managing a recovery phrase? If not, learn first before moving to self-custody.

Only download wallets from official websites or app stores, and buy hardware wallets from the manufacturer or an authorized seller.

Essential safety rules

  • Enable two-factor authentication on exchange accounts.
  • Beware of fake apps, fake support accounts and phishing links.
  • Always double-check the address before sending funds, and try a small test transaction first.
  • Keep your phone and computer updated. Our guide on why your phone is slow also covers updates.

Crypto is volatile and risky. Only invest money you can afford to lose, after building an emergency fund and setting a clear monthly budget. For personal advice, speak with a qualified financial professional.

Summary

A crypto wallet stores the private keys that give access to your coins on the blockchain. Custodial wallets are convenient but depend on a platform, while self-custody gives you full control and full responsibility. Protect your recovery phrase offline, use a hardware wallet for larger amounts and always stay alert to scams.
